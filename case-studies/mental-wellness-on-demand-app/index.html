Quick answer

What did Stack18 deliver for the wellness startup?

Stack18 took a venture-backed startup's student mental wellness product from MVP scope and design to store release on both Android and iOS. The whole project, from scope to launch, took three months.

The brief

An app students would trust, on a venture-backed clock.

Mental wellness is personal, and students are a demanding audience. The product had to feel simple and safe, and it had to ship fast.

01

Reaching students where they are

Students live on their phones. The app had to feel quick, simple and welcoming from the very first screen.

02

Trust and privacy

Mental wellness is a sensitive subject, so every part of the experience had to be designed with care for the user.

03

Two platforms, one deadline

Android and iOS had to launch together, in three months, to keep pace with a venture-backed plan.

Our approach

From scope to store release in three months.

1

Scope the MVP

We agreed the smallest version of the product that could prove the idea with real students.

2

Design

Clickable prototypes were reviewed and refined before any engineering began.

3

Build

Android and iOS built in two-week sprints, with test builds on real devices along the way.

4

Launch

Release to both app stores, ready for the startup to learn from real usage.

What we built

A product built around the student experience.

Designed to be easy to start, easy to return to and easy for the startup team to run.

01

Student experience

An app that students can pick up in moments.

  • Quick, welcoming onboarding
  • On-demand access to wellness support
  • A calm, accessible interface
02

Engagement

Gentle prompts that help students come back.

  • Reminders and notifications
  • A consistent experience on Android and iOS
03

Admin console

Tools for the startup’s own team.

  • User and content management
  • Visibility into how the product is used
04

Foundation for growth

A base the startup can keep building on.

  • Ready for new features after launch
  • Built to evolve with student feedback
The outcome

What the project delivered.

Two platforms, one launch

Android and iOS released together in three months, from the first scoping conversation to the app stores.

A foundation to build on

Built so the startup can keep adding features and learning from how students actually use the app.

Lessons for similar projects

What other teams can take from this.

Practical points from the brief and the way the project was run, for anyone planning something similar.

  • Scope the smallest useful versionAgreeing the MVP up front is what made a three-month, two-platform launch possible.
  • Prototype before engineeringReviewing clickable prototypes first removes rework later, especially for sensitive experiences.
  • Design for trust from the first screenFor personal subjects such as wellness, privacy and tone are product features, not polish.
  • Ship both stores togetherOne build process for Android and iOS keeps features and timelines in step.
