Quick answer

What is Stack18 Agentic SDLC?

Agentic SDLC is Stack18’s AI-native software delivery model. Ten specialised AI agents carry work through seven stages, from discovery to operations, while your people approve requirements, architecture, releases and production changes at five governance gates. It is available today as a managed Stack18 engagement, with a self-serve platform to follow.

An agentic software development life cycle (agentic SDLC) is one where AI agents execute most lifecycle tasks, such as writing specs, code, tests and pipelines, and humans set intent, define constraints and approve what carries risk. New to the idea? Read what the Agentic SDLC is.

Why it matters

Software delivery is fragmented, and AI has made it faster, not safer.

Most teams have added AI to one stage of the lifecycle. Agentic SDLC applies it to all seven, under one set of rules.

Context is lost at every handoff

Requirements live in documents, designs drift from code and decisions cannot be traced back to the business goal.

Every stage is a silo

Planning, design, repositories, pipelines and monitoring sit in separate tools, so status is reported by hand.

AI tools stop at the editor

Coding assistants speed up typing, but requirements, testing, release and operations stay manual.

Speed without control

Agents run with no shared oversight, and audit trails are reconstructed when someone asks for them.

TodayWith Agentic SDLC
  • Requirements in a document nobody updates→Requirements traced to code, tests and releases
  • Status meetings and spreadsheets→One live command centre for every project and agent
  • AI used ad hoc by individuals→Ten agents with defined roles, permissions and costs
  • Approvals in email threads→Five gates with named approvers and recorded decisions
  • Documentation written at the end→Living documentation produced as the work happens

How it works

Discover to operate, with a human gate before anything that carries risk.

Each stage takes a structured input, is executed by agents, produces a traceable output and, where it matters, waits for a named person to approve before the next stage starts.

  1. 01

    Discover

    Compass

    Synthesises goals, context and constraints into stakeholders, risks and open questions.

    OutputIntent brief

    Intent gateScope, goals and success measures confirmed
  2. 02

    Define

    Draft

    Drafts the PRD, user stories and acceptance criteria, and flags conflicts and gaps.

    OutputRequirements set

    Requirements gateAcceptance criteria approved
  3. 03

    Design

    Blueprint · Frame · Grid

    Proposes architecture, data model, API contracts and UX flows, with costs and trade-offs.

    OutputArchitecture and design specs

    Architecture gateDesign and data decisions signed off
  4. 04

    Build

    Weld · Grid

    Writes code, integrations and tests as reviewable pull requests against the approved spec.

    OutputWorking code with tests

    Continuous checks, no separate gate
  5. 05

    Test

    Watch

    Runs functional, regression, performance and security checks and triages failures.

    OutputQuality and security report

    Release gateQuality and security evidence reviewed
  6. 06

    Deploy

    Launch

    Builds pipelines, provisions environments and stages releases with a rollback plan.

    OutputRelease candidate

    Production gatePromotion approved by the right owner
  7. 07

    Operate

    Pulse

    Monitors health, flags regressions and turns production signals into the next backlog.

    OutputInsights for the next cycle

    Continuous checks, no separate gate

Gates are configurable per project, team and risk level. A low-risk internal tool can run with fewer sign-offs than a payments system; every decision is recorded either way.

FAQ

Agentic SDLC questions, answered.

Contracts, the self-serve platform, control, data and results. Anything else, ask a senior specialist on a free call.

Talk to Stack18
What is Stack18 Agentic SDLC?

Agentic SDLC is Stack18’s AI-native way to deliver software. Ten specialised AI agents do the work across seven stages, from discovery to operations, and your people approve every critical decision at five governance gates. Everything is visible in one command centre.

How can we use Agentic SDLC today?

Through a managed engagement with Stack18. You can start with a fixed-scope project, such as one product or one modernisation, or take an Enterprise Transformation Pod on a 12, 24 or 36-month annual contract. In both cases Stack18 runs the agents and your team approves the gates.

How does the enterprise contract work?

It starts with a free 60-minute briefing, a 2–4 week fixed-fee assessment and a 1–2 week pod proposal. After signature, the pod is staffed within 30 days. You commit to pod-months for the year, can flex within an agreed band each quarter and can link part of the fee to scorecard targets.

What is included in an Enterprise Transformation Pod contract?

A dedicated multidisciplinary team, Agentic SDLC with its 10 agents, access to the command centre for your approvers, delivery governance, quarterly business reviews and Stack18 solution modules at enterprise rates. Cloud and AI model usage are billed at cost and reported monthly.

Who owns the code and documents?

You do. Code, specifications, tests, documentation and audit evidence are your property, team members work under NDAs and IP assignment, and every contract includes a documented handover plan.

When will the self-serve platform launch?

The self-serve platform is in development and the waitlist is open. Managed clients use the command centre first. Waitlist members are invited to a private beta in stages, and launch dates and pricing will be announced to the waitlist first.

How will self-serve pricing work?

Final prices are announced at launch. The planned model is a workspace subscription plus metered agent usage, with unlimited viewers and approvers, so you are not charged for every stakeholder who signs off.

Is Agentic SDLC an AI coding assistant?

No. Coding is one of seven stages. Agentic SDLC covers intent, requirements, architecture, design, code, testing, release and operations, and keeps them traceable to each other with governance across all of them.

Can AI agents push code to production on their own?

No. Agents open pull requests and stage releases, but they cannot merge to protected branches or promote to production. Those actions need the human approvals you configure at the release and production gates.

Does it work with our existing tools?

Yes. Agentic SDLC connects to your Git hosting, CI/CD, cloud, issue tracker, identity provider and observability tools rather than replacing them.

Which AI models do the agents use, and is our data used for training?

Agents use leading commercial or open-weight models chosen per task, and the model can be changed. Your code and data are never used to train models for anyone else, and enterprise deployments can run in your own cloud account.

How do you measure whether it works?

We baseline and report lead time for changes, deployment frequency, change failure rate and time to recover, plus requirement-to-test traceability and cost per delivered feature, at every quarterly review.

Can we start with one project?

Yes. Many clients start with a single product or modernisation as a fixed-scope project, then move to an Enterprise Transformation Pod once the approach has proved itself.

Can IT services firms and agencies use it for their own clients?

That is one of the main uses of the self-serve platform. Service providers will be able to run client engagements, software and agents in one workspace, with client approvers invited at the gates. Join the waitlist and choose “Service provider or agency”.
