    Quick answer

    What is Project Management Software?

    Project management software is a system for planning, assigning and tracking work. It stores tasks and projects, shows them in views such as lists, boards, timelines and calendars, and often adds automation rules, reporting dashboards and AI assistance.

    Reviewed by the Stack18 editorial team

    Key takeaways

    • 27 profiles in this category, listed alphabetically and not ranked.
    • 20 of 27 offer a free plan or program.
    • $4–$20 reported entry price per user per month. Confirm current pricing with each vendor.
    • Check first: Views and fit for your method, Automation limits, Pricing, seat minimums and add-ons.
    • Sourcing: 12 vendor-sourced and 11 independent-source profiles, each with dated links.

    Project management software gives teams a shared place to plan work, assign owners and deadlines, and see progress without chasing updates through email and chat. Modern tools combine tasks with several views, automation, dashboards and increasingly AI.

    Products range from simple Kanban boards to platforms with portfolios, goals, workload planning and enterprise security. Pricing is usually per user, with free plans and clear tiers, but seat minimums, automation limits and AI add-ons change the real cost.

    The profiles below summarise independent sources, with dates and caveats. They are research aids, not rankings, and they do not replace a trial with your own projects.

    How to choose

    How to choose Project Management Software: a step-by-step approach.

    1. 1

      Define how your team works

      Decide whether you run Kanban, sprints, timelines or a mix, and which views people need every day.

    2. 2

      List must-have limits

      Write down the automations, guests, storage and reporting you need, and check which plan includes them.

    3. 3

      Price the full team

      Count paid seats, any seat minimum, AI add-ons and the likely headcount over two years.

    4. 4

      Check integrations

      Confirm connectors for chat, email, calendar, files and your engineering or CRM tools.

    5. 5

      Trial with a real project

      Move one live project into each shortlisted tool and see how quickly the team adopts it.

    6. 6

      Plan for governance

      Check SSO, permissions and export options before rolling out across the company.

    Common mistakes

    Common mistakes when choosing Project Management Software.

    Picking on the free plan alone

    Free plans often cap automations, views or boards, so check the limits against your real workload.

    Ignoring seat minimums

    A three-seat minimum changes the cost for very small teams.

    Underestimating AI cost

    AI may be bundled, capped by credits or sold per member, which changes the total.

    Over-building workflows

    Complex automations and fields created too early often go unused.

    Skipping change management

    A tool only works if the team adopts it, so agree on conventions before rollout.

    Glossary

    Project Management Software key terms explained.

    Kanban board
    A board of columns and cards that shows work moving through stages.
    Gantt or timeline view
    A view that shows tasks over time with dependencies and deadlines.
    Portfolio
    A group of related projects tracked together for status and priorities.
    Workload management
    Viewing how much work is assigned to each person to avoid overload.
    Automation rule
    A trigger-and-action pair that performs routine steps, such as assigning a task when a status changes.
    Seat
    A paid licence for one user. Some vendors require a minimum number of seats.

    FAQ

    Project Management Software questions and answers

    What is the best free project management software?

    20 of the 27 project management software profiles here document a free plan: Airtable, Asana, Basecamp, ClickUp, Hive, Jira, Linear, monday.com and 12 more. Free plans and tiers usually cap users, records or usage, so check each profile for limits. The list is alphabetical, not a ranking.

    What is the best project management software for small businesses?

    Products documented mainly for small and mid-size teams include Basecamp, Hive, Paymo, Plane, Productive, Shortcut, Taiga, Todoist and 2 more. Basecamp may suit agencies and client-service teams of 20 or more that want flat pricing. Hive may suit small teams that want a free project management tool with AI. Paymo may suit freelancers and agencies that bill by time and need invoicing in the same tool. The best fit depends on your sales or work process and budget, so trial two or three.

    Which project management software suits large enterprises?

    13 profiles are documented for large enterprises: Airtable, Asana, ClickUp, Jira, Linear, monday.com, OpenProject, ProofHub and 5 more. Larger rollouts should also check SSO, permissions, audit logs, data residency and contract terms on each profile.

    Which project management software has built-in AI features?

    18 of 27 profiles document an AI assistant, agents or AI features: Airtable, Asana, ClickUp, Hive, Jira, Linear, monday.com, OpenProject and 10 more. Check which plans include AI and whether agent or credit usage is billed separately.

    Is there self-hosted or open-source project management software?

    Yes. OpenProject, Plane, Redmine and Taiga document a self-hosted deployment option. Self-hosting gives more control over data but adds hosting, upgrade and security work.

    Where can I find alternatives to a specific project management tool?

    Every product in this category has its own alternatives guide, for example Asana alternatives, ClickUp alternatives and monday.com alternatives. Each guide compares the main competitors on pricing, free plan, features and fit.

    What is the difference between project management and work management software?

    Project management tools focus on planned projects with deadlines and milestones. Work management tools cover broader ongoing work, such as requests and workflows. Many products now do both.

    Is free project management software enough?

    For small teams with simple needs, often yes. Free plans usually limit automations, views, storage or boards, so check the limits against your workload.

    Why do project management prices differ between sources?

    Vendors change plans, run promotions and price by region, seat count and billing period. Confirm current prices on the vendor's pricing page.

    Should I choose a tool with built-in AI?

    Check what the AI does, which plans include it, whether credits are capped and whether it is billed per seat or per use.
