    Quick answer

    What is Accounting Software?

    Accounting software records a business's financial transactions and turns them into books and reports. It typically handles invoicing, bills, bank feeds and reconciliation, sales tax or VAT, and financial statements, with payroll, inventory, projects and multi-entity consolidation on higher plans or in mid-market products.

    Reviewed by the Stack18 editorial team

    Key takeaways

    • 17 profiles in this category, listed alphabetically and not ranked.
    • 4 of 17 offer a free plan.
    • $15–$38 reported entry price per month at the smallest paid list size. Confirm current pricing with each vendor.
    • Check first: Local tax compliance, Total cost including payroll and add-ons, Your accountant's preferred software.
    • Sourcing: 2 profiles cite the vendor's own pages and 8 rely on independent sources only, all with dated links.

    Accounting software replaces spreadsheets and manual ledgers with double-entry books that stay in sync with your bank, your invoices and your accountant. Most small-business tools are cloud subscriptions; some markets, such as India, still rely heavily on desktop licences.

    The category spans free invoicing tools for freelancers, small-business platforms such as QuickBooks Online, Xero, Zoho Books and Sage Accounting, and mid-market financial systems and ERPs such as Sage Intacct, NetSuite and Odoo that add multi-entity consolidation, revenue recognition and inventory.

    Local tax compliance often decides the shortlist: Making Tax Digital in the UK, GST, e-invoicing and e-way bills in India, and sales tax and 1099s in the US. The profiles below summarise dated sources; they are research aids, not rankings.

    Key features

    Key features and capabilities of Accounting Software.

    The capabilities that define the category. Not every product offers all of them, and depth varies a lot between entry-level and enterprise tools.

    • General ledger

      Double-entry books with a configurable chart of accounts.

    • Bank feeds and reconciliation

    How to choose

    How to choose Accounting Software: a step-by-step approach.

    1. 1

      Start with compliance

      List the tax filings and statutory reports you must produce and rule out tools that cannot handle them in your country.

    2. 2

      Ask your accountant

      The software your accountant already uses can save time and fees at year end.

    3. 3

      Map your transactions

      Count invoices, bills, bank accounts, currencies, entities and stock items, and check plan limits against them.

    4. 4

      Price the full stack

      Add payroll, payments, time tracking, extra users and implementation to the subscription price.

    5. 5

      Trial with real data

      Import a month of real transactions and reconcile a bank account before committing.

    6. 6

      Plan the cut-over

      Switch at a month, quarter or year end and keep read-only access to the old system for audits.

    • Does it file returns in our country, including e-invoicing if required?
    • Which of our banks connect automatically?

    • Can our accountant access it at no extra cost?

    Common mistakes

    Common mistakes when choosing Accounting Software.

    Ignoring local compliance

    A popular global tool may not file your country's returns or e-invoices.

    Comparing only the plan price

    Payroll, payments and add-on fees often cost more than the subscription.

    Switching mid-year

    Changing systems mid-period complicates opening balances and year-end reports.

    Buying ERP too early

    Mid-market systems carry large implementation costs that small businesses rarely need.

    Leaving the accountant out

    An accountant who cannot work in your system will charge more or ask for exports.

    Glossary

    Accounting Software key terms explained.

    Double-entry accounting
    Recording every transaction as equal debits and credits so the books always balance.
    Bank reconciliation
    Matching transactions in your books to your bank statement or feed.
    Chart of accounts
    The list of accounts used to categorise every transaction.
    Accounts payable and receivable
    Money you owe suppliers and money customers owe you.
    Multi-entity consolidation
    Combining the books of several companies into group reports, removing intercompany transactions.
    Revenue recognition
    Recording revenue when it is earned, which matters for subscriptions and long projects.

    FAQ

    Accounting Software questions and answers

    What is the best free accounting software?

    4 of the 17 accounting software profiles here document a free plan: Odoo Accounting, Sage Accounting, Wave and Zoho Books. Free plans and tiers usually cap users, records or usage, so check each profile for limits. The list is alphabetical, not a ranking.

    What is the best accounting software for small businesses?

    Products documented mainly for small and mid-size teams include FreshBooks, QuickBooks Online, Sage Accounting, TallyPrime, Wave, Xero and Zoho Books. FreshBooks may suit freelancers and service businesses that bill clients by invoice. QuickBooks Online may suit US small businesses that want the most widely used cloud accounting tool. Sage Accounting may suit UK small businesses that need Making Tax Digital-ready accounting. The best fit depends on your sales or work process and budget, so trial two or three.

    Which accounting software suits large enterprises?

    3 profiles are documented for large enterprises: Odoo Accounting, Oracle NetSuite and Sage Intacct. Larger rollouts should also check SSO, permissions, audit logs, data residency and contract terms on each profile.

    Which accounting software has built-in AI features?

    4 of 17 profiles document an AI assistant, agents or AI features: QuickBooks Online, Sage Accounting, Sage Intacct and TallyPrime. Check which plans include AI and whether agent or credit usage is billed separately.

    Which accounting software includes inventory management?

    7 of 17 profiles document inventory or stock management: Odoo Accounting, Oracle NetSuite, QuickBooks Online, Sage Accounting, Sage Intacct, TallyPrime and Zoho Books. On several products it starts on a higher plan, so check each profile.

    Is there self-hosted or open-source accounting software?

    Yes. Odoo Accounting documents a self-hosted deployment option. Self-hosting gives more control over data but adds hosting, upgrade and security work.

    Where can I find alternatives to a specific accounting platform?

    Every product in this category has its own alternatives guide, for example QuickBooks Online alternatives, Xero alternatives and Zoho Books alternatives. Each guide compares the main competitors on pricing, free plan, features and fit.

    What is the best accounting software for a small business?

    It depends on your country, your accountant and whether you need inventory or payroll. QuickBooks Online and Xero are widely used, Zoho Books and Wave offer free plans, and local tools such as TallyPrime or Sage Accounting fit specific tax regimes.

    When should a business move to an ERP such as NetSuite or Sage Intacct?

    Usually when it runs several entities, needs revenue recognition or advanced controls, or has outgrown small-business plan limits, and can absorb implementation costs that often start around $25,000.

    Is free accounting software good enough?

    For freelancers and very small businesses, often yes. Free plans usually limit users, bank feeds or invoices, so check what moves to paid plans.

    Why do accounting software prices change so often?

    Vendors adjust plans and run steep introductory discounts; in 2026 QuickBooks Online and Sage Accounting raised prices and Wave moved features to its paid plan. Confirm current prices with the vendor.
