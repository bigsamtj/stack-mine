    Quick answer

    What is Collaboration Software?

    Collaboration software for knowledge work is a shared workspace where teams create documents, wikis and knowledge bases, comment together, control access and search across content. Many tools add AI that searches and summarises company knowledge.

    Also called Team Collaboration

    Reviewed by the Stack18 editorial team

    Key takeaways

    • 29 profiles in this category, listed alphabetically and not ranked.
    • 15 of 29 offer a free plan or program.
    • $1.8–$13.33 reported entry price per user per month. Confirm current pricing with each vendor.
    • Check first: Structure versus flexibility, Search and AI, Pricing and guests.
    • Sourcing: 7 vendor-sourced and 11 independent-source profiles, each with dated links.

    Collaboration software gives teams a shared place to write documents, maintain a wiki and keep knowledge searchable, so answers do not live only in chat threads or individual files. Many products now add AI search and agents over workspace content.

    Tools differ in structure and flexibility. Some use structured spaces and page trees suited to documentation, while others offer free-form pages and databases that can also host light project tracking. Pricing is usually per user, with free guests or free plans in some cases.

    The profiles below summarise independent sources, with dates and caveats. They are research aids, not rankings, and a trial with your own content is the best test.

    Common mistakes

    Common mistakes when choosing Collaboration Software.

    Creating a content dump

    Without ownership and structure, a wiki fills with stale pages that nobody trusts.

    Ignoring search

    A knowledge base is only valuable if people can find answers quickly.

    Assuming AI is included

    AI may be capped by credits or limited to higher plans, so check allowances.

    Forgetting security add-ons

    SSO and provisioning may need a separate subscription.

    Skipping migration planning

    Moving existing content takes time, so plan for imports and clean-up.

    Glossary

    Collaboration Software key terms explained.

    Wiki
    A collaborative set of linked pages that a team edits and maintains together.
    Knowledge base
    An organised collection of answers and documentation for staff or customers.
    Space
    A container that groups related pages and controls who can access them.
    Database (in a workspace)
    A structured set of records, often shown as tables or boards, inside a document workspace.
    AI credits
    A usage allowance for AI features such as search, chat and agents, often reset monthly.
    Guest
    An outside collaborator with limited access to specific content.

    Collaboration Software questions and answers

    What is the best free collaboration software?

    15 of the 29 collaboration software profiles here document a free plan: Coda (Superhuman Docs), Confluence, Lark, Microsoft Teams, Miro, Mural, Notion, Nuclino and 7 more. Free plans and tiers usually cap users, records or usage, so check each profile for limits. The list is alphabetical, not a ranking.

    What is the best collaboration software for small businesses?

    Products documented mainly for small and mid-size teams include Nuclino, Pumble, Twist and Zoho Cliq. Nuclino may suit small teams that want a minimal internal wiki at a low price. Pumble may suit startups and small teams that want free team chat with unlimited history. Twist may suit remote and async-first teams across time zones. The best fit depends on your sales or work process and budget, so trial two or three.

    Which collaboration software suits large enterprises?

    14 profiles are documented for large enterprises: Coda (Superhuman Docs), Confluence, Google Workspace, Lark, Mattermost, Microsoft Teams, Miro, Mural and 6 more. Larger rollouts should also check SSO, permissions, audit logs, data residency and contract terms on each profile.

    Which collaboration software has built-in AI features?

    15 of 29 profiles document an AI assistant, agents or AI features: Coda (Superhuman Docs), Confluence, Google Workspace, Lark, Mattermost, Microsoft Teams, Miro, Mural and 7 more. Check which plans include AI and whether agent or credit usage is billed separately.

    Is there self-hosted or open-source collaboration software?

    Yes. Mattermost, Rocket.Chat and Zulip document a self-hosted deployment option. Self-hosting gives more control over data but adds hosting, upgrade and security work.

    Where can I find alternatives to a specific collaboration tool?

    Every product in this category has its own alternatives guide, for example Microsoft Teams alternatives, Notion alternatives and Slack alternatives. Each guide compares the main competitors on pricing, free plan, features and fit.

    What is the difference between a wiki and a document editor?

    A wiki organises linked pages into a shared, evolving knowledge base, while a document editor focuses on individual files. Many workspaces combine both.

    Can one tool replace both a wiki and project management?

    Flexible workspaces can host light project databases, but heavy project operations usually fit purpose-built tools better. Decide what your main need is.

    Why do prices differ between sources?

    Vendors use volume bands, regional pricing and changing AI bundles. Confirm current prices on the vendor's pricing page.

    How should I test AI search?

    Ask real questions about your own content and check whether answers are correct, cited and permission-aware.
