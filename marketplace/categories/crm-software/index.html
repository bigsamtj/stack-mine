    Quick answer

    What is CRM Software?

    CRM software is a system of record for customer-facing work: it stores contacts, companies, conversations and deals, tracks pipeline stages, and automates routine follow-up. Most products add reporting, forecasting, email and calendar sync, and increasingly AI assistants that draft messages or summarise activity.

    Reviewed by the Stack18 editorial team

    Key takeaways

    • 30 profiles in this category, listed alphabetically and not ranked.
    • 10 of 30 offer a free plan or program.
    • $7–$65 reported entry price per user per month. Confirm current pricing with each vendor.
    • Check first: Pricing model and total cost, Customisation depth, Automation and reporting limits.
    • Sourcing: 14 vendor-sourced and 10 independent-source profiles, each with dated links.

    Customer relationship management (CRM) software gives sales, marketing and service teams one shared record of every contact, company and deal, so that follow-ups, forecasts and handovers do not depend on spreadsheets or individual inboxes.

    The CRM market ranges from free, lightweight tools that organise contacts and a single pipeline to configurable platforms that model complex sales processes, territories and approvals. The right choice depends less on feature counts than on how your team sells, how many people need full access, and how much configuration you can support.

    Evaluation criteria

    Features and criteria to evaluate in CRM Software.

    01

    Pricing model and total cost

    Check whether pricing is per seat, per module or per contact, which seat types exist, and whether onboarding or implementation fees apply.

    02

    Customisation depth

    Look for custom fields and objects, record types and permission models, and note which plan unlocks each.

    03

    Automation and reporting limits

    Workflow, sequence and report allowances often differ sharply between plans.

    04

    Integrations and ecosystem

    Confirm native connectors for your email, calendar, telephony, billing and support tools, and the availability of an API.

    05

    AI capabilities

    Separate AI assistants included in plans from agents or add-ons billed separately, and check what data they can access.

    06

    Administration and security

    Review roles and permissions, SSO options, audit capabilities and data-handling documentation.

    How to choose

    How to choose CRM Software: a step-by-step approach.

    1. 1

      Map how your team sells

      Write down your pipeline stages, handoffs and who needs full access versus view-only access. A CRM should fit that process, not the other way round.

    2. 2

      List your must-have data and customisation

      Decide which custom fields, objects, record types and permissions you need, then check which plan unlocks each one.

    3. 3

      Price the real setup

      Count seats by type, onboarding fees, add-ons and AI usage, and model your headcount over the next two years rather than today's.

    4. 4

      Check integrations and the API

      Confirm connectors for your email, calendar, phone, billing and support tools, and whether the API limits on your plan fit your needs.

    5. 5

      Trial with your own data

      Import a sample of real contacts and deals, rebuild one workflow, and have two or three reps use it for a week before committing.

    6. 6

      Plan for exit

      Check data export options, contract terms and how hard it would be to move your data and automation later.

    Questions to ask before you buy

    • Does it fit our sales process without heavy customisation?
    • What is the full per-user cost with the features we need?
    • How does it sync with email, calendar and marketing tools?
    • How hard is it to migrate our data?
    • Which AI and agent features are included or extra?
    Common mistakes

    Common mistakes when choosing CRM Software.

    Choosing on headline price

    Seat types, onboarding fees and add-ons can change the total cost far more than the entry price.

    Over-customising too early

    Heavy configuration before the sales process is stable creates rework and slows adoption.

    Ignoring data quality

    Reports, forecasts and AI features are only as good as the data your team enters consistently.

    Skipping the integration check

    A CRM that does not connect to the tools your team already uses leads to manual entry and incomplete records.

    Underestimating adoption

    A powerful CRM that reps avoid delivers little value, so involve users in the trial and plan training.

    Glossary

    CRM Software key terms explained.

    Pipeline
    The set of stages a deal moves through from first contact to closed, used to track progress and forecast revenue.
    Lead scoring
    A method of ranking leads by how likely they are to buy, using fit and behaviour signals, sometimes with AI.
    Custom objects
    User-defined record types, beyond standard contacts, companies and deals, used to model data specific to your business.
    Sequence
    A series of scheduled follow-up emails or tasks sent to a prospect until they reply or meet.
    Sales forecasting
    Projecting expected revenue from the deals in the pipeline, usually weighted by stage or probability.
    Seat
    A licence for one user. Vendors may offer different seat types with different levels of access and price.

    FAQ

    CRM Software questions and answers

    What is the best free CRM software?

    10 of the 30 CRM software profiles here document a free plan: Agile CRM, Attio, Bigin by Zoho CRM, Bitrix24, Capsule CRM, EngageBay, Freshsales, HubSpot and 2 more. Free plans and tiers usually cap users, records or usage, so check each profile for limits. The list is alphabetical, not a ranking.

    What is the best CRM software for small businesses?

    Products documented mainly for small and mid-size teams include Agile CRM, Bigin by Zoho CRM, Bitrix24, Capsule CRM, Close CRM, Copper, EngageBay, folk and 9 more. Agile CRM may suit small teams that want a free CRM for up to 10 users. Bigin by Zoho CRM may suit solo founders and small teams that want a simple pipeline CRM at a very low price. Bitrix24 may suit teams of 10 to 100 that want CRM, tasks and chat in one low-cost platform. The best fit depends on your sales or work process and budget, so trial two or three.

    Which CRM software suits large enterprises?

    7 profiles are documented for large enterprises: Attio, Freshsales, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales, Salesforce Sales Cloud, SugarCRM and Zoho CRM. Larger rollouts should also check SSO, permissions, audit logs, data residency and contract terms on each profile.

    Which CRM software has built-in AI features?

    21 of 30 profiles document an AI assistant, agents or AI features: Attio, Bigin by Zoho CRM, Bitrix24, Capsule CRM, Close CRM, EngageBay, folk, Freshsales and 13 more. Check which plans include AI and whether agent or credit usage is billed separately.

    Is there self-hosted or open-source CRM software?

    Yes. Bitrix24 and SugarCRM document a self-hosted deployment option. Self-hosting gives more control over data but adds hosting, upgrade and security work.

    Where can I find alternatives to a specific CRM?

    Every product in this category has its own alternatives guide, for example HubSpot alternatives, Pipedrive alternatives and Zoho CRM alternatives. Each guide compares the main competitors on pricing, free plan, features and fit.

    What is the difference between a CRM and a spreadsheet?

    A CRM links every contact, company, email and deal in one shared record, enforces pipeline stages, and automates reminders and reporting. A spreadsheet can store the same fields but does not keep activity history, permissions or automation consistent across a team.

    Do small teams need an enterprise CRM?

    Not necessarily. Teams with one pipeline and a handful of users often start with a free or low-cost plan and upgrade when they need automation, forecasting or custom data models. The cost of switching later is worth weighing early.

    Why do CRM prices differ so much between sources?

    Vendors change plans, run promotions, and price by seat type, module, region and billing period. Third-party summaries often disagree for this reason, so confirm current prices on the vendor's own pricing page.

    Should I choose a CRM with built-in AI?

    Check what the AI does, which plans include it, and whether agent or credit-based usage is billed separately. AI features are most useful when your CRM data is already clean and consistently entered.
