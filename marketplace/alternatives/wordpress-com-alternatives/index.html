Quick answer

What are the best alternatives to WordPress.com?

The main alternatives to WordPress.com covered here are Webflow, Ghost, Framer and Duda. Webflow may suit design-led teams building custom marketing sites, while Ghost may suit publishers running newsletters and paid memberships. Switching

How to switch from WordPress.com.

Moving off WordPress.com means exporting content and media, rebuilding templates or front ends, re-creating forms and integrations, and mapping 301 redirects for every URL so search rankings carry over. Keep the old site live until redirects and metadata are verified. Rebuild one high-traffic page and one blog post in each shortlisted CMS, then have a marketer publish an update without developer help.

Switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your WordPress.com data and test an import into the new product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Compare total cost for your real seat count, including add-ons and onboarding fees.
  4. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  5. Check contract terms, renewal dates and notice periods with WordPress.com.

General guidance, not specific to any vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

FAQ

WordPress.com alternatives: questions and answers

What is the best alternative to WordPress.com?

No single alternative is best for everyone. The alternatives covered here are Webflow, Ghost, Framer and Duda. Webflow may suit design-led teams building custom marketing sites. Ghost may suit publishers running newsletters and paid memberships. Framer may suit designers and startups shipping polished sites fast. Test two or three against your own process before deciding.

Is there a free alternative to WordPress.com?

Yes. Webflow, Ghost and Framer list a free plan or free tier in their sourced profiles. Free plans usually cap users, records or usage, so check the limits on each profile before relying on one.

Which WordPress.com alternative is best for small teams?

Webflow, Ghost, Framer and Duda are documented for small teams. Webflow may suit design-led teams building custom marketing sites. Ghost may suit publishers running newsletters and paid memberships. Framer may suit designers and startups shipping polished sites fast.

Which WordPress.com alternatives suit larger organisations?

Webflow is documented for large enterprises as well as smaller teams. Larger rollouts should also check SSO, permissions, audit logs and contract terms on each profile.

Why do teams switch from WordPress.com?

The limitations documented on the WordPress.com profile are: Free plan shows ads and blocks plugins. Developer tools need Business. Monthly billing costs far more. Whether they matter depends on your team and budget.

Who should stay with WordPress.com?

WordPress.com may still suit bloggers and businesses wanting hosted WordPress without server work; writers and publishers; small stores on Commerce.
