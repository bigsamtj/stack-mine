Quick answer

What are the best alternatives to Zulip?

The main alternatives to Zulip covered here are Slack, Microsoft Teams, Mattermost and Pumble. Slack may suit teams that want real-time channel-based communication, while Microsoft Teams may suit organisations already using Microsoft 365. None is best for everyone: the right collaboration tool depends on team size, budget and which Zulip limitation you are trying to solve.

Teams usually start looking beyond Zulip for documented reasons: the Cloud Free plan limits file storage to 5 GB in total and, according to independent listings, searchable history to 10,000 messages; cloud Plus has a 10-user minimum, and single sign-on and SCIM are reported only on higher plans. Zulip remains a reasonable fit for distributed teams that want asynchronous chat organised by topic, so an alternative is not automatically better, only different.

Below, every option is compared with Zulip in one table, then described with its strengths, trade-offs and pricing, each taken from its own sourced profile. Anything the sources do not document is marked as unknown.

Switching

How to switch from Zulip.

Moving off Zulip usually means exporting messages, pages and files, checking how links, mentions and permissions survive the import, and rebuilding integrations and bots. Agree a cut-over date so conversations do not split across two tools. Move one team and its everyday channels or spaces into each shortlisted tool for two weeks.

Switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your Zulip data and test an import into the new product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Compare total cost for your real seat count, including add-ons and onboarding fees.
  4. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  5. Check contract terms, renewal dates and notice periods with Zulip.

General guidance, not specific to any vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

FAQ

Zulip alternatives: questions and answers

What is the best alternative to Zulip?

No single alternative is best for everyone. The alternatives covered here are Slack, Microsoft Teams, Mattermost and Pumble. Slack may suit teams that want real-time channel-based communication. Microsoft Teams may suit organisations already using Microsoft 365. Mattermost may suit regulated or security-conscious organisations that need to control where data lives. Test two or three against your own process before deciding.

Is there a free alternative to Zulip?

Yes. Slack, Microsoft Teams and Pumble list a free plan or free tier in their sourced profiles. Free plans usually cap users, records or usage, so check the limits on each profile before relying on one.

Which Zulip alternative is best for small teams?

Slack, Microsoft Teams and Pumble are documented for small teams. Slack may suit teams that want real-time channel-based communication. Microsoft Teams may suit organisations already using Microsoft 365. Pumble may suit startups and small teams that want free team chat with unlimited history.

Which Zulip alternatives suit larger organisations?

Slack, Microsoft Teams and Mattermost are documented for large enterprises as well as smaller teams. Larger rollouts should also check SSO, permissions, audit logs and contract terms on each profile.

Is there a self-hosted or open-source alternative to Zulip?

Mattermost documents a self-hosted deployment option. Self-hosting gives more control over data but adds hosting, upgrade and security work.

Why do teams switch from Zulip?

The limitations documented on the Zulip profile are: The Cloud Free plan limits file storage to 5 GB in total and, according to independent listings, searchable history to 10,000 messages. Cloud Plus has a 10-user minimum, and single sign-on and SCIM are reported only on higher plans. Self-hosting means the organisation runs, secures and upgrades its own server. Whether they matter depends on your team and budget.

Who should stay with Zulip?

Zulip may still suit distributed teams that want asynchronous chat organised by topic; open-source projects, universities and research groups; organisations that want open-source chat they can self-host.
