Quick answer

What is Drupal?

Drupal is a free, open-source content management system released under the GPL that powers complex, high-traffic and multilingual websites, with structured content types, granular permissions and an API-first architecture, and Drupal CMS 2.0 adding a visual page builder and site templates.

  • PricingFree and open source; hosting and development costs vary.

Overview

Drupal overview: who it is for and what it does.

Drupal is used by governments, universities, media groups and large enterprises that need structured content, many editors with different permissions, multilingual sites and integrations through APIs. It is maintained by a global community and the Drupal Association.

Drupal CMS 2.0, released in early 2026 on Drupal core 11.3, made the Canvas visual page builder the default editor and introduced site templates, starting with Byte for SaaS marketing sites. Optional AI tools can draft pages from prompts, help administrators create content types through a chatbot and suggest image alt text for human review.

The software is free. Costs come from hosting, development, security updates and maintenance, which usually require developers or an agency. Drupal suits organisations whose content model and governance needs outgrow simpler site builders.

Main problem it solves

Lets organisations run large, structured, multilingual sites with fine-grained editorial control on software they own.

Typical customer segment

Enterprises, public sector, higher education, media and non-profits with complex content needs.

Target users

  • Content editors working in a governed workflow
  • Developers building custom modules and headless front ends
  • Site owners managing many languages or sites

Primary use cases

  • Large corporate and government websites
  • University and media portals
  • Multilingual sites
  • Headless CMS via JSON:API
  • Intranets and community sites

What makes it distinct

  • Structured content types and fields in core.
  • Granular roles and permissions for large editorial teams.
  • Visual Canvas builder and optional AI in Drupal CMS 2.0.

FAQ

Drupal questions and answers

What is Drupal used for?

Drupal is used for large corporate and government websites, university and media portals, multilingual sites and headless CMS via JSON:API.

Does Drupal have a free plan?

Yes. Yes (open source). Free trial: Not applicable.

How much does Drupal cost?

Free and open source; hosting and development costs vary. Drupal itself is free.

What are the main alternatives to Drupal?

The main alternatives to Drupal covered on Stack18 are WordPress.com, Joomla, Contentful and Strapi. WordPress.com may suit bloggers and businesses wanting hosted WordPress without server work. Joomla may suit multilingual sites on a budget. Contentful may suit enterprise content operations delivering to many channels. See the Drupal alternatives guide for a side-by-side table.

What integrations does Drupal support?

Documented integrations include JSON:API and REST, Mailchimp recipe in Drupal CMS, SSO via contributed modules and Search (Solr, Elasticsearch) via modules. API: JSON:API and REST built into core.

What are the limitations of Drupal?

Needs developers for setup, hosting and upgrades. Steeper learning curve than hosted site builders. AI tools are optional and need configuration and review.

Which platforms does Drupal run on?

Drupal is documented as available on: Self-hosted (PHP, Linux), Managed Drupal hosting.
