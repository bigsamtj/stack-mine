Quick answer

What is Asana?

Asana is a work management and project management platform from Asana, Inc. that organises tasks, projects, workflows, portfolios and goals, sold per user in Personal, Starter, Advanced, Enterprise and Enterprise+ plans.

  • Best forTeams that want a free start and a clear upgrade path
  • Team size fitSmall teams, Mid-size teams, Large enterprises
  • PricingFree Personal plan; Starter reported at $10.99 per user per month billed annually. Free plan available.

Overview

Asana overview: who it is for and what it does.

Asana helps teams plan and track work as tasks and projects, with multiple views, rules-based automation, reporting and, on higher plans, portfolios, goals and workload management. Independent pricing guides describe a free Personal plan for small teams and paid Starter and Advanced plans priced per user.

The plans are described as a clear ladder. Starter adds timeline views, a workflow builder, forms, custom fields and unlimited dashboards; Advanced adds portfolios, goals, workload management, approvals and multi-step rules, with a much higher monthly automation allowance; Enterprise and Enterprise+ add security and administration such as SSO, SCIM, audit logs and key management.

Asana has also added AI. Guides report AI features in the paid tiers, with AI Studio credits bundled per tier. Paid plans have a seat minimum and no single-user option, so very small buyers should check the minimum cost.

Main problem it solves

Gives teams one place to plan work, assign owners and deadlines, automate routine steps and report on progress across projects and goals.

Typical customer segment

Individuals and small teams on the free plan, mid-size teams on Starter and Advanced, and large organisations on Enterprise plans.

Target users

  • Small teams starting with a free project tool
  • Cross-functional teams that need timelines, forms and automation
  • Larger organisations that need portfolios, goals, workload views and enterprise security

Primary use cases

  • Project planning with timelines and Gantt-style views
  • Request intake with forms
  • Workflow automation with rules
  • Portfolio and goal tracking
  • Workload and resource planning

What makes it distinct

  • A free Personal plan, then a clear Starter, Advanced and Enterprise ladder.
  • Portfolios, goals and workload management on higher plans for cross-team planning.
  • Automation allowances and AI credits that scale by plan.

Strengths, limitations and best fit

Asana pros and cons.

Strengths, limitations and best fit. Each point cites a source; editorial summaries are not customer reviews.

Strengths

  • A free Personal plan lets small teams start without paying. [1]
  • A clear plan ladder ties specific capabilities, such as timelines, portfolios and goals, to named tiers. [3]
  • Automation and AI allowances scale by plan, with unlimited automations on Enterprise. [5]
  • Enterprise plans add SSO, SCIM and audit logs for organisations with security requirements. [3]

Limitations

  • Paid plans have a seat minimum and no single-user option. [2]
  • Per-user pricing grows quickly with team size, and Advanced costs more than twice Starter. [5]
  • Key reporting, workload and goal features are limited to higher-tier plans. [1]
  • Starter rules are single-trigger, so complex multi-step automation needs Advanced. [4]

Best for

  • Teams that want a free start and a clear upgrade path
  • Cross-functional teams that need timelines, forms and automation
  • Organisations that need portfolios, goals and workload planning

May be less suited to

  • Solo users who want a paid single-seat plan
  • Teams that need advanced multi-step automation but only want to pay for Starter

FAQ

Asana questions and answers

What is Asana used for?

Asana is used to plan and track work as tasks and projects, automate workflows, manage portfolios and goals, and report on progress across teams.

Does Asana have a free plan?

Yes. Asana offers a free Personal plan for small teams. Paid plans start at Starter.

How much does Asana cost?

Independent guides report Starter at $10.99 per user per month billed annually ($13.49 monthly) and Advanced at $24.99 ($30.49 monthly), with Enterprise and Enterprise+ priced on request. Confirm on asana.com.

What is the difference between Asana Starter and Advanced?

Starter adds timelines, a workflow builder, forms and unlimited dashboards. Advanced adds portfolios, goals, workload management, approvals and multi-step rules, with a much larger automation allowance.

What are the main alternatives to Asana?

The main alternatives to Asana covered on Stack18 are monday.com, ClickUp, Trello and Jira. monday.com may suit teams that want a flexible visual board-based tool. ClickUp may suit teams that want a generous free plan to evaluate. Trello may suit small teams that want simple Kanban boards. See the Asana alternatives guide for a side-by-side table.

What integrations does Asana support?

Documented integrations and ecosystem items include 100+ integrations (Starter, reported), Salesforce and SAP (Advanced, reported). API details: Not reviewed for this profile.

What are the limitations of Asana?

Paid plans have a seat minimum and no single-user option. Per-user pricing grows quickly with team size, and Advanced costs more than twice Starter. Key reporting, workload and goal features are limited to higher-tier plans.

Which platforms does Asana run on?

Asana is documented as available on: Web, Mobile apps.
