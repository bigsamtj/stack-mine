Overview

Confluence overview: who it is for and what it does.

Confluence is a team workspace for documentation, wikis and knowledge bases, and part of the Atlassian family alongside Jira. Independent guides describe a Free plan for up to ten users with unlimited pages and spaces, and paid Standard, Premium and Enterprise tiers priced per user per month.

Plans differ by scale and capability. Standard is reported to offer 250 GB of storage, automation and guest access of up to five guests per paid user; Premium adds analytics, team calendars, advanced admin features, unlimited storage and stronger support; Enterprise is custom-priced and adds stronger security and a cheaper per-user rate than Premium plus identity add-ons at high user counts.

AI is bundled through Rovo, Atlassian's AI platform for search, chat and agents. Several sources report monthly credit allowances of about 25, 70 and 150 per user on Standard, Premium and Enterprise, with the Free plan excluded. SSO and user provisioning on lower tiers need the separate Atlassian Guard subscription, so total cost depends on the security features you need.

Scope: This profile covers Confluence Cloud. Confluence Data Center is licensed separately.

Main problem it solves

Gives teams a structured, searchable home for documentation and knowledge that connects to Jira and the rest of the Atlassian suite.

Typical customer segment

Small teams on the Free plan, growing teams on Standard and Premium, and large organisations on Enterprise.

Target users

  • Teams that use Jira and want connected documentation
  • Organisations that need an internal wiki and knowledge base
  • Larger companies that need strong permissions and administration

Primary use cases

  • Internal wikis and knowledge bases
  • Product and engineering documentation
  • Team spaces and project documentation
  • AI search across documentation with Rovo
  • Meeting notes and decision logs

What makes it distinct

  • Deep integration with Jira and the Atlassian ecosystem.
  • A structured page-and-space model suited to documentation.
  • Rovo AI credits bundled into paid plans.

Strengths, limitations and best fit

Confluence pros and cons.

Strengths, limitations and best fit. Each point cites a source; editorial summaries are not customer reviews.

Strengths

  • A free plan for up to ten users with unlimited pages and spaces. [1]
  • Rovo AI search, chat and agents are bundled into paid plans through monthly credits. [4]
  • Tight integration with Jira and other Atlassian products suits engineering and product teams. [3]

Limitations

  • Per-user pricing scales linearly, so cost rises noticeably for large teams. [1]
  • SSO and provisioning on Free, Standard and Premium need a separate Atlassian Guard subscription. [5]
  • Rovo credit allowances are finite, and heavier AI use consumes them quickly. [2]
  • Published per-user prices differ between sources because of volume bands and regional pricing. [5]

FAQ

Confluence questions and answers

What is Confluence used for?

Confluence is used to create and organise documentation, wikis and knowledge bases in structured spaces, and to connect them to Jira work.

Does Confluence have a free plan?

Yes. Independent guides report a Free plan for up to ten users with unlimited pages and spaces.

How much does Confluence cost?

Four independent guides report Standard at $5.42 and Premium at $10.44 per user per month billed annually, with Enterprise custom. Other sources show different figures, so confirm with Atlassian.

Is Confluence's AI included?

Paid plans bundle Rovo AI through monthly credits of about 25 (Standard), 70 (Premium) and 150 (Enterprise) per user, according to independent guides. The Free plan does not include full Rovo.

What are the main alternatives to Confluence?

The main alternatives to Confluence covered on Stack18 are Notion, Slack and Jira. Notion may suit teams that want a flexible wiki with a light project layer. Slack may suit teams that want real-time channel-based communication. Jira may suit Agile software development teams. See the Confluence alternatives guide for a side-by-side table.

What integrations does Confluence support?

Documented integrations and ecosystem items include Jira and the Atlassian suite, Atlassian Guard (SSO, SCIM), Rovo across Atlassian apps. API details: Not reviewed for this profile.

What are the limitations of Confluence?

Per-user pricing scales linearly, so cost rises noticeably for large teams. SSO and provisioning on Free, Standard and Premium need a separate Atlassian Guard subscription. Rovo credit allowances are finite, and heavier AI use consumes them quickly.

Which platforms does Confluence run on?

Confluence is documented as available on: Web, Mobile apps.
