Overview

Coda (Superhuman Docs) overview: who it is for and what it does.

Coda is a document and workspace tool where a doc can hold prose, relational tables, buttons, formulas and automations in one place. Teams use it for wikis, project trackers, planning docs, lightweight CRMs and internal tools, and it competes with Notion, Confluence and Airtable more than with traditional word processors.

Its most distinctive commercial feature is Doc Maker billing. Superhuman's help centre states that you pay only for Doc Makers, meaning people who create or structure docs, while editors and viewers are free on every plan. Workspace admins count as Doc Makers, and upgrading a free workspace needs only one paid Doc Maker.

The product changed names in 2026. Independent reports say Coda became Superhuman Docs on 8 July 2026, that coda.io/pricing now redirects to Superhuman's plans page, and that the old Team plan is now called Business. Sources describe the Pro and Business plans as bundling Grammarly and Go, with Mail added on Business, and report higher list prices than before the rename.

Scope: Coda was renamed Superhuman Docs on 8 July 2026 and now sits inside the Superhuman suite with Grammarly, Mail and Go. This profile covers the docs product, which many buyers still search for as Coda.

Main problem it solves

Replaces separate documents, spreadsheets and trackers with interactive docs that hold text, data and automation together.

Typical customer segment

Individuals and small teams on Free and Pro, growing teams on Business, and organisations rolling out docs across departments on Enterprise.

Target users

  • Teams that want wikis, trackers and planning docs in one tool
  • Product, operations and marketing teams building lightweight internal apps
  • Organisations with many viewers and few doc builders

Primary use cases

  • Team wikis and knowledge bases
  • Project and roadmap trackers
  • Meeting notes and planning docs
  • Lightweight internal tools
  • Cross-tool dashboards using Packs

What makes it distinct

  • Maker billing: only Doc Makers pay, while editors and viewers are free.
  • Docs that combine text, relational tables, formulas and automations.
  • A large library of Packs for connecting other tools (about 600 reported).

Strengths, limitations and best fit

Coda (Superhuman Docs) pros and cons.

Strengths, limitations and best fit. Each point cites a source; editorial summaries are not customer reviews.

Strengths

  • Only Doc Makers are billed. Editors and viewers are free on every plan, which suits organisations with many readers and few builders. [1]
  • A doc can combine prose, tables, formulas and automations, so one tool can cover a wiki and a lightweight tracker. [3]
  • Existing documents carry over after the rename and workspaces keep working. [2]
  • Paid plans bundle Grammarly and Superhuman Go, adding writing and AI assistance without a separate purchase. [3]
  • A free plan lets individuals and small groups try the product without a deadline. [4]

Limitations

  • List prices rose with the rename: Pro moved from about $10 to $12 annually and the equivalent of Team from about $30 to $33. [2]
  • The Doc Maker versus editor distinction adds admin overhead, and workspace admins count as paid Doc Makers. [1]
  • The rename and move into the Superhuman suite means pricing pages, links and plan names have changed, and older articles quote outdated numbers. [3]
  • Moving docs from an original Coda workspace into a Superhuman workspace is a manual step buyers must choose to take. [2]
  • Per-Doc-Maker cost can exceed per-member tools such as Notion when most of the team builds docs. [4]

Best for

  • Teams that want docs, wikis and trackers in one workspace
  • Organisations with many readers and a small group of builders
  • Existing Coda teams deciding how to handle the Superhuman rename

May be less suited to

  • Teams where nearly everyone builds docs and per-builder pricing adds up
  • Buyers who want an enterprise wiki with a long-established admin model such as Confluence
  • Teams that need a fixed price list that has not recently changed

FAQ

Coda (Superhuman Docs) questions and answers

What is Coda?

Coda is a collaborative document platform where a doc can include text, tables, formulas, automations and integrations called Packs. Teams use it for wikis, trackers and internal tools. It was renamed Superhuman Docs in July 2026.

Is Coda now Superhuman Docs?

Yes. Superhuman's help centre states that on 8 July 2026 Coda became Superhuman Docs, joining Grammarly, Mail and Go in the Superhuman suite. Existing docs carry over, and the old coda.io pricing page redirects to Superhuman.

How much does Coda cost?

After the rename, independent guides report Pro at $12 and Business at $33 per Doc Maker per month on annual billing, or about $15 and $40 monthly. Free is $0 and Enterprise is custom. Confirm on superhuman.com.

What is a Doc Maker in Coda?

A Doc Maker is someone who creates or structures docs. Superhuman states that only Doc Makers are billed, editors and viewers are free on every plan, and workspace admins also count as Doc Makers.

Is Coda free?

Yes, there is a Free plan, with reported limits such as 1 GB of attachments per doc. Upgrading a free workspace requires paying for only one Doc Maker.

Coda vs Notion: which is better?

Coda is stronger for docs that behave like apps, with formulas and automations, and charges only for Doc Makers. Notion is a broader general workspace with per-member pricing. See the Notion profile and the Notion vs Confluence comparison for context.

What are the main alternatives to Coda?

Notion, Confluence, Airtable and Miro are common alternatives, depending on whether you need a wiki, a database or a visual workspace.

What integrations does Coda (Superhuman Docs) support?

Documented integrations and ecosystem items include Packs (about 600 reported), Grammarly, Superhuman Go, Superhuman Mail (Business and above). API details: Not reviewed for this profile.

What are the limitations of Coda (Superhuman Docs)?

List prices rose with the rename: Pro moved from about $10 to $12 annually and the equivalent of Team from about $30 to $33. The Doc Maker versus editor distinction adds admin overhead, and workspace admins count as paid Doc Makers. The rename and move into the Superhuman suite means pricing pages, links and plan names have changed, and older articles quote outdated numbers.

Which platforms does Coda (Superhuman Docs) run on?

Coda (Superhuman Docs) is documented as available on: Web, Mobile apps.

What are the main alternatives to Coda (Superhuman Docs)?

The main alternatives to Coda (Superhuman Docs) covered on Stack18 are Notion, Confluence, Airtable and Miro. Notion may suit teams that want a flexible wiki with a light project layer. Confluence may suit teams that already use Jira and want connected documentation. Airtable may suit teams that want custom trackers and workflows over linked data.
