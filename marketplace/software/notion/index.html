Quick answer

What is Notion?

Notion is a connected workspace from Notion Labs that combines documents, wikis, databases and project tracking, sold per member in Free, Plus, Business and Enterprise plans.

  • Best forTeams that want a flexible wiki with a light project layer
  • Team size fitSmall teams, Mid-size teams, Large enterprises
  • PricingFree plan; paid plans billed per member (Plus, Business) with AI bundled from Business. Free plan available.
  • Main limitationEvery editor is a billed member, so seat counts can grow quickly.
  • AlternativesConfluence, Slack · compare all

Reviewed by the Stack18 editorial team · 10 sources

Overview

Notion overview: who it is for and what it does.

Notion brings documents, wikis, databases and lightweight project management into one workspace. Independent pricing guides describe four plans: Free, Plus, Business and Enterprise, billed per member, with guests free and annual billing lowering the per-seat cost.

AI is a major part of the 2026 plan structure. Several sources, one citing Notion's own comparison table, report that full Notion AI, including Notion Agent, AI Meeting Notes and Enterprise Search in beta, is bundled into Business and Enterprise, while Free and Plus offer only a limited trial. Older guides describe AI as a separate add-on, so the bundling has changed and buyers should check the current page.

Notion suits teams that want a flexible wiki with a light project layer. Business reportedly adds SAML SSO, private teamspaces and longer page history, and Enterprise adds an audit log and advanced controls. Published prices differ between guides, so this profile does not record exact figures.

Main problem it solves

Gives teams one flexible place to write documents, keep a wiki, track projects and search company knowledge, instead of using several separate tools.

Typical customer segment

Individuals and small teams on Free and Plus, multi-team organisations on Business, and large organisations on Enterprise.

Target users

  • Individuals and small teams that want a flexible wiki and docs
  • Teams that want a light project layer inside their knowledge base
  • Organisations that want AI search and agents over workspace content

Primary use cases

  • Team wikis and knowledge bases
  • Documents and meeting notes
  • Lightweight project and task databases
  • AI search across workspace content
  • Company handbooks and onboarding

What makes it distinct

  • Docs, wiki and databases in one flexible workspace.
  • Guests are free, so outside collaborators do not add seat cost.
  • AI features such as Notion Agent bundled into Business and Enterprise.

Strengths, limitations and best fit

Notion pros and cons.

Strengths, limitations and best fit. Each point cites a source; editorial summaries are not customer reviews.

Strengths

  • A free plan and free guests make it easy for individuals and small teams to start. [2]
  • Full Notion AI is reported as bundled into Business, rather than a separate add-on. [1]
  • Flexible docs, wikis and databases suit teams that want a wiki with a light project layer. [3]

Limitations

  • Every editor is a billed member, so seat counts can grow quickly. [3]
  • Free and Plus include only a limited AI trial, so meaningful AI use needs Business or above. [1]
  • Guides disagree on prices and on how AI is bundled, so budgeting needs a check of the current page. [4]
  • Some guides note that flexible databases are lighter than purpose-built project tools for heavy operations. [3]

Best for

  • Teams that want a flexible wiki with a light project layer
  • Individuals and small teams starting on a free plan
  • Organisations that want AI search over workspace knowledge

May be less suited to

FAQ

Notion questions and answers

What is Notion used for?

Notion is used for documents, wikis, knowledge bases, project and task databases, and AI search across workspace content.

Does Notion have a free plan?

Yes. Notion offers a Free plan, which includes a limited trial of Notion AI.

Is Notion AI included in paid plans?

Several 2026 sources report that full Notion AI is bundled into Business and Enterprise, while Free and Plus include a limited trial. Older guides describe AI as a separate add-on, so check Notion's current pricing page.

How much does Notion cost?

Guides disagree: Plus is reported between $10 and $12 per member per month and Business between $18 and $24 on annual billing. No price is recorded here. Confirm on notion.com/pricing.

What are the main alternatives to Notion?

The main alternatives to Notion covered on Stack18 are Confluence and Slack. Confluence may suit teams that already use Jira and want connected documentation. Slack may suit teams that want real-time channel-based communication. See the Notion alternatives guide for a side-by-side table.

What integrations does Notion support?

Documented integrations and ecosystem items include Enterprise Search across connected content (beta). API details: Not reviewed for this profile.

What are the limitations of Notion?

Every editor is a billed member, so seat counts can grow quickly. Free and Plus include only a limited AI trial, so meaningful AI use needs Business or above. Guides disagree on prices and on how AI is bundled, so budgeting needs a check of the current page.

Which platforms does Notion run on?

Notion is documented as available on: Web, Desktop apps, Mobile apps.
