Overview

Miro overview: who it is for and what it does.

Miro is an online whiteboard where distributed teams brainstorm, map processes, run workshops and plan work on an infinite canvas. Independent guides describe a Free plan with three editable boards, unlimited members, 7,000+ templates and 250+ integrations, and paid plans that remove the board cap and add collaboration, security and AI features. Miro is used for design thinking, agile planning, diagramming, retrospectives and visual documentation.

Pricing is per member. Guides report Starter at $8 per member per month billed annually ($10 monthly) and Business at $20 annually ($25 monthly), with Enterprise custom-quoted and requiring at least 30 members. Starter adds unlimited private boards, visitor editing and high-resolution exports. Business adds unlimited workspaces and guests, a library of 4,700+ diagramming shapes, polls and two-way integrations, and Enterprise adds governance and security controls.

AI is metered through credits. Reported allowances are 25 AI credits per member per month on Starter and 50 on Business, with a limited AI trial on Free, and a reported add-on of $20 per month for 350 extra credits. Business and Enterprise also include Miro MCP, which connects boards with AI tools. Teams comparing whiteboards often look at FigJam, Whimsical and Mural, so check which seats and AI limits you actually need.

Scope: This profile covers Miro's visual collaboration platform. AI credit add-ons, Miro Prototypes and Enterprise-only modules such as Engage, Insights and Portfolios are priced separately.

Main problem it solves

Lets distributed teams think, plan and decide together on a shared visual canvas instead of scattered slides, documents and calls.

Typical customer segment

Small teams on the Free and Starter plans, scaling teams on Business, and large organisations on Enterprise with a 30-member minimum.

Target users

  • Product, design and engineering teams running workshops and planning
  • Agile coaches and facilitators
  • Consultants and agencies working with clients
  • Enterprises that want governance over visual collaboration

Primary use cases

  • Brainstorming and workshops
  • Agile planning and retrospectives
  • Process mapping and diagramming
  • Customer journey and user story mapping
  • Product and UX design collaboration
  • Visualising codebases and architecture with Miro MCP
  • Client workshops using visitor and guest access
  • AI-assisted diagram and summary generation

What makes it distinct

  • A large template library of 7,000+ templates and 250+ integrations on the Free plan
  • Visitor and guest access so outsiders can collaborate without paid seats
  • Per-member AI credits that rise by plan, plus Miro MCP on Business and Enterprise
  • Enterprise governance, SCIM and data residency options

FAQ

Miro questions and answers

What is Miro?

Miro is an online visual collaboration platform built around an infinite whiteboard. Teams use it for brainstorming, workshops, agile planning, diagramming and product design, with templates, integrations and AI features. It is sold per member in Free, Starter, Business and Enterprise plans.

How much does Miro cost per member per month?

Independent guides report Free at $0, Starter at $8 and Business at $20 per member per month billed annually. Monthly billing is reported at $10 and $25. Enterprise is custom-quoted with a minimum of 30 members.

Is Miro free?

Yes. Miro has a free plan with three editable boards, unlimited members, 7,000+ templates, 250+ integrations and a limited AI allowance. Teams that need more boards move to Starter at $8 per member per month billed annually.

What is the difference between Miro Starter and Business?

Starter ($8) removes the board cap and adds unlimited private boards, visitor editing, high-resolution exports, timers, voting and 25 AI credits per member. Business ($20) adds unlimited workspaces and guests, 4,700+ shapes, polls, two-way integrations, AI workflows, Miro MCP and 50 AI credits.

Does Miro have AI features?

Yes. Miro includes AI credits per member on paid plans, 25 on Starter and 50 on Business, and a limited trial on Free. A reported add-on provides 350 extra credits for $20 per month. Miro MCP connects boards to AI tools on Business and Enterprise.

How much would Miro cost for 10 members?

At reported annual prices, 10 members on Starter cost $80 per month ($960 a year) and on Business $200 per month ($2,400 a year). Visitors and guests are free. AI credit add-ons and Enterprise features are extra.

What is Miro MCP?

Miro MCP is a Model Context Protocol integration that connects Miro boards with AI tools, for example to visualise codebases. Guides report it is available on Business and Enterprise, and one source lists a limit of 2,000 daily MCP calls on Business.

Is Miro better than FigJam or Mural?

Miro is a full whiteboarding platform with a large template and integration library. FigJam is reported to be included with every Figma seat, and Mural starts at a higher entry price in one comparison. Match the tool to your design stack and workshop needs.

What are the best alternatives to Miro?

Within this directory, Notion and Confluence cover documentation and knowledge work, and Microsoft Teams covers chat and meetings. Dedicated whiteboard alternatives include FigJam, Whimsical and Mural. See the linked profiles for details.

What integrations does Miro support?

Documented integrations and ecosystem items include Jira, Asana, 250+ integrations on Free, Two-way integrations on Business, Miro MCP for AI tools. API details: API details were not reviewed for this profile.

What are the limitations of Miro?

The Free plan allows only three editable boards, and older boards lock. Business at $20 is 2.5 times the Starter price, a large jump for growing teams. AI is credit-limited, and extra credits cost about $20 per month for 350 credits.

Which platforms does Miro run on?

Miro is documented as available on: Web.

The main alternatives to Miro covered on Stack18 are Notion, Confluence and Microsoft Teams. Notion may suit teams that want a flexible wiki with a light project layer. Confluence may suit teams that already use Jira and want connected documentation. Microsoft Teams may suit organisations already using Microsoft 365.
