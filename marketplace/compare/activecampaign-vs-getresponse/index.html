Quick answer

ActiveCampaign vs GetResponse: the short answer

ActiveCampaign is an email marketing and marketing automation platform sold in Starter, Plus, Pro and Enterprise plans priced by contact count, with CRM and messaging add-ons. GetResponse is an email marketing and automation platform with landing pages, webinars and course tools, sold in Free, Starter, Marketer, Creator and Enterprise (MAX) plans. Both are email marketing platforms, but they differ in pricing model, target team size and focus.

On pricing, ActiveCampaign is described as: monthly or yearly subscription priced by contact count, with add-ons for CRM, users and messaging; GetResponse as: monthly or annual subscription priced by contact count, with about 18% off annually. GetResponse lists a free plan. ActiveCampaign does not document a permanent free plan. ActiveCampaign is positioned for teams that need deep automation and segmentation, and GetResponse for marketers who want email, funnels and webinars in one tool.

Everything below is drawn from the two sourced profiles and the 4 dated sources listed at the end. Where a source does not document something it is marked Unknown, and third-party estimates are labelled. No winner is declared: the better fit depends on your size, budget and requirements.

  • GetResponse: Free for up to 500 contacts; Starter reported at $19 per month or $15.58 billed annually (1,000 contacts).
  • Free plan: ActiveCampaign — No; GetResponse — Yes.

  • GetResponse best for: Marketers who want email, funnels and webinars in one tool.

Considerations

ActiveCampaign vs GetResponse: pricing model, ecosystem and fit.

Pricing model and cost drivers

ActiveCampaign: monthly or yearly subscription priced by contact count, with add-ons for CRM, users and messaging. At 10,000 contacts one guide reports about $149 (Starter), $189 (Plus), $375 (Pro) and $589 (Enterprise) a month billed yearly. ActiveCampaign's pricing page is dynamic and was not reviewed directly. GetResponse: monthly or annual subscription priced by contact count, with about 18% off annually. Plan names have changed over time, and some guides use older names such as Email Marketing and Marketing Automation. GetResponse's own pricing page was not reviewed directly. Model costs at your real seat count, usage and contract term before comparing headline prices.

What sets each apart

The ActiveCampaign profile highlights: a deep automation builder available from the entry plan, with limits on Starter; contact scoring and custom objects from Plus; predictive and AI features from Pro. The GetResponse profile highlights: webinar hosting and a course creator on the Creator plan; unlimited email sends on paid plans; AI content generators from Starter.

Integrations and ecosystem

ActiveCampaign integrations named in its sources: CRM and pipelines (add-on), SMS and WhatsApp (add-ons), Postmark transactional email (add-on) and API (from Starter). GetResponse: Webinar and course tools (built in) and Transactional email and SMS on Enterprise. Check that the connectors you rely on exist on the plan you would buy.

Deployment and team fit

ActiveCampaign runs on Web and is documented for small teams, mid-size teams and large enterprises. GetResponse runs on Web and is documented for small teams, mid-size teams and large enterprises. We did not test setup or migration.

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • Automation is available from the entry plan, with unlimited actions from Plus.
  • Scoring, custom objects and advanced segmentation support sophisticated targeting.

Limitations

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • A free plan covers up to 500 contacts, with premium features for the first 14 days.
  • Paid plans include unlimited email sends.
  • Webinars, courses and funnels are built in rather than bought separately.
Limitations
  • Inactive contacts count toward your list size and tier.
  • Starter allows only one custom automation workflow.
  • Webinars are limited to 10 attendees on Free and need Creator for 100.

Switching

Switching between ActiveCampaign and GetResponse.

Moving off ActiveCampaign or GetResponse usually means exporting subscribers with their tags, segments and consent records, rebuilding automations and sign-up forms, recreating templates, and authenticating your sending domain on the new platform. Warm up sending gradually and keep the old account until the first campaigns land well. Send one real campaign and build one real automation in each shortlisted platform, then compare inbox placement and reporting.

General switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your data and test an import into the other product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Compare total cost at your real volume, including add-ons and services.
  4. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  5. Check contract terms and notice periods with both vendors.

General guidance, not specific to either vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

What is the difference between ActiveCampaign and GetResponse?

ActiveCampaign is an email marketing and marketing automation platform sold in Starter, Plus, Pro and Enterprise plans priced by contact count, with CRM and messaging add-ons. GetResponse is an email marketing and automation platform with landing pages, webinars and course tools, sold in Free, Starter, Marketer, Creator and Enterprise (MAX) plans. ActiveCampaign is positioned for teams that need deep automation and segmentation; GetResponse for marketers who want email, funnels and webinars in one tool.

Is ActiveCampaign cheaper than GetResponse?

It depends on your volume and plan. ActiveCampaign: No free plan; plans reported from $15 per month billed yearly (Starter, 1,000 contacts). GetResponse: Free for up to 500 contacts; Starter reported at $19 per month or $15.58 billed annually (1,000 contacts). Compare both at your real seat count, usage and contract term.

Do ActiveCampaign or GetResponse have a free plan?

GetResponse lists a free plan. ActiveCampaign does not document a permanent free plan. ActiveCampaign free trial: Yes (14 days). GetResponse free trial: Yes (premium features for 14 days).

ActiveCampaign or GetResponse: which is better for small teams?

ActiveCampaign and GetResponse are both documented for small teams. ActiveCampaign suits teams that need deep automation and segmentation; GetResponse suits marketers who want email, funnels and webinars in one tool. Neither is best for everyone.

What are the main limitations of ActiveCampaign and GetResponse?

How much does GetResponse cost?
