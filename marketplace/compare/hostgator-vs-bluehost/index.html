HostGator vs Bluehost: the short answer

HostGator and Bluehost are budget hosts owned by Newfold Digital. Both sell shared WordPress hosting with low intro prices and higher renewals.

HostGator allows multiple sites from its entry plan; Bluehost is often recommended for WordPress beginners.

Switching

Switching between HostGator and Bluehost.

Moving between HostGator and Bluehost means migrating files and databases and updating DNS. Test on a temporary URL before switching.

General switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your data and test an import into the other product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  4. Check contract terms and notice periods with both vendors.

General guidance, not specific to either vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

FAQ

HostGator vs Bluehost: questions and answers

What is the difference between HostGator and Bluehost?

HostGator and Bluehost are budget hosts owned by Newfold Digital. Both sell shared WordPress hosting with low intro prices and higher renewals. HostGator allows multiple sites from its entry plan; Bluehost is often recommended for WordPress beginners.

Are HostGator and Bluehost the same company?

Both are owned by Newfold Digital, but they are separate brands with different plans.

Which is cheaper?

Intro prices are close: HostGator Hatchling $3.75 and Bluehost Basic $3.99 a month on 36-month terms.

Do both renew at higher prices?

Yes. Renewals are much higher than intro prices for both.

Which is better for small businesses, HostGator or Bluehost?

HostGator is documented for solo and freelancers, small teams; Bluehost for small teams, mid-size teams. Choose HostGator if you need several small sites on an entry plan; choose Bluehost if you are a WordPress beginner.

What are the alternatives to HostGator and Bluehost?

Both are listed in Stack18’s Web Hosting directory. See the HostGator alternatives guide and the Bluehost alternatives guide for sourced shortlists of competitors.
