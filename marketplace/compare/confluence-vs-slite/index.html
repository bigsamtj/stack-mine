Quick answer

Confluence vs Slite: the short answer

Confluence and Slite are both team wikis and knowledge bases, but they target different organisations. Confluence, from Atlassian, is a structured wiki sold per user in Free, Standard, Premium and Enterprise plans, tightly linked to Jira. Slite is a lighter knowledge base built around verified documentation and AI answers, sold per user in Basic, Pro and Enterprise plans with no permanent free plan.

The sources reviewed describe the choice as ecosystem against simplicity. Confluence is described as the stronger platform for Atlassian-heavy and larger teams, with a free plan and low entry prices. Slite is described as the cleaner, more modern option for small and mid-size teams that want AI question answering and verified docs, including non-engineering teams.

We compare what Slite's own pages and independent 2026 sources support and mark the rest Unknown. Several sources are competitors of one or both products, so we treat their opinions as such and rely mainly on published plan details. No winner is declared: the better fit depends on your existing tools and team size.

Confluence is cheaper at every comparable tier. Our arithmetic on reported annual prices for 50 users gives about $3,252 a year on Confluence Standard ($5.42 x 50 x 12) against $6,000 on Slite Basic ($10 x 50 x 12), and about $6,264 on Confluence Premium ($10.44 x 50 x 12) against $12,000 on Slite Pro ($20 x 50 x 12). Confluence's per-user rate slides down in volume bands, so larger teams may pay less per user.

A free start favours Confluence: up to 10 users cost nothing, while Slite offers only a 14-day trial. Teams that need AI from day one should compare allowances rather than prices. Confluence ties Rovo credits to the plan, while Slite includes Ask AI questions on Basic and agent credits on Pro.

One competitor guide cautions that Confluence's cost stacks with Atlassian Guard and Marketplace apps, so price the complete configuration you need. Prices changed on the Confluence side in late 2025, so confirm current terms with both vendors.

Considerations

Confluence vs Slite: implementation, ecosystem, AI and security.

Ease of implementation and usability

We did not test setup or migration for either product. Guides describe Slite as a lighter tool that non-engineering teams adopt quickly, and Confluence as stronger where the wiki must sit next to Jira and structured spaces. Pilot with one team's real documentation in each.

Integrations and ecosystem

Confluence connects to Jira and the Atlassian Marketplace, which is why Atlassian-heavy teams favour it. Slite lists search across connected tools on Pro and API and MCP access on Basic. Integration depth was not assessed for either, so check the apps you need.

AI capabilities

Confluence's AI runs on Rovo credits that rise by plan. Slite's Ask AI answers questions from your docs and cites the sources, with monthly question limits on Basic and agent credits on Pro. Slite's own pages claim a self-maintaining knowledge base, which is a vendor claim we did not test. Output quality was not tested for either product.

Security, deployment and administration

Slite lists OpenID SSO on Pro and SCIM, audit logs and an SLA on Enterprise. Confluence offers stronger security on Enterprise and SSO through Atlassian Guard. We did not review certifications or data residency, so request current security documentation from both vendors.

Strengths and limitations

Confluence and Slite: each option, in balance.

C

Confluence

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • A free plan for up to ten users with unlimited pages and spaces.
  • Rovo AI search, chat and agents are bundled into paid plans through monthly credits.
  • Tight integration with Jira and other Atlassian products suits engineering and product teams.
Limitations
  • Per-user pricing scales linearly, so cost rises noticeably for large teams.
  • SSO and provisioning on Free, Standard and Premium need a separate Atlassian Guard subscription.
  • Rovo credit allowances are finite, and heavier AI use consumes them quickly.
  • Published per-user prices differ between sources because of volume bands and regional pricing.
S

Slite

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • Ask AI search and answers, doc verification and the Knowledge Management Panel are included from the Basic plan.
  • A public API and MCP access are available on every plan.
  • SOC 2 Type II compliance and automatic backups are listed on every plan.
  • A 14-day free trial with no credit card lets teams try Basic and Pro before paying.
Limitations
  • There is no permanent free plan on the current pricing page.
  • AI use is metered: 30 monthly questions and 30 AI edits per seat on Basic, and 50 credits per seat for the agent on Pro.
  • Single sign-on needs Pro, and SCIM, audit logs and an SLA need Enterprise.
  • Older guides quote earlier prices, so published comparisons may not match the current plans.

Switching

Switching between Confluence and Slite.

Moving between Confluence and Slite means exporting pages and attachments, rebuilding the space and permissions structure, and recreating Jira macros and templates, which have no Slite equivalent. Pilot one team's docs and check search and permissions before moving the rest.

General switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your data and test an import into the other product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  4. Check contract terms and notice periods with both vendors.

General guidance, not specific to either vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

FAQ

Confluence vs Slite: questions and answers

Is Confluence cheaper than Slite?

Yes at every comparable tier: Standard is about $5.42 per user per month against $10 for Slite Basic, and Premium is about $10.44 against $20 for Slite Pro, billed annually. Confluence also has a free plan for up to 10 users.

Does Slite have a free plan?

No permanent free plan. Slite offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card.

Which has better AI?

Sources lean towards Slite for AI question answering over docs and towards Confluence for teams in the Atlassian ecosystem. Quality was not tested here.

Which is better for Jira users?

Confluence, which links directly to Jira and the Atlassian Marketplace.

Does Slite verify documents?

Yes. Slite lists doc verification and a Knowledge Management Panel on Basic, and doc fact-checking on Pro.

Which has SSO on a lower plan?

Slite lists OpenID SSO on Pro at $20. Confluence uses Atlassian Guard, a separate subscription outside Enterprise.

Which suits non-technical teams better?

Guides describe Slite as designed so finance, people and customer teams adopt it alongside engineers, though that comes from Slite's own material.
