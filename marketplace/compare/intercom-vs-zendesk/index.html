Quick answer

Intercom vs Zendesk: the short answer

Intercom and Zendesk are two of the most evaluated customer support platforms for teams that want AI in the loop. Intercom, now branded Fin, is a messaging-first suite with a shared inbox, Messenger and help center, sold per seat with its Fin AI Agent billed per outcome. Zendesk is a ticketing-first platform with an omnichannel Suite, sold per agent with AI agents billed per automated resolution beyond an allowance.

The sources reviewed agree on the headline difference. Intercom publishes a single AI rate of $0.99 per resolution and is described as strongest for chat inside a product. Zendesk is described as stronger for phone, ticket volume and a larger marketplace, but its per-resolution AI rate is not shown on its pricing page and is reported at roughly $1.50 to $2.00. Both therefore have two meters: seats and AI resolutions.

We compare what independent 2026 sources support and mark the rest Unknown. Seat prices differ between sources, particularly for Intercom's entry plan. No winner is declared: the better fit depends on your channels, your expected AI volume and how much governance you need.

All Zendesk plans and sources

At the entry level Zendesk looks cheaper: Support Team is reported at $19 per agent per month against $29 to $39 per seat for Intercom Essential, depending on the source. But the two entry plans buy different things. Zendesk's $19 plan is ticketing only, while omnichannel messaging and AI agents start at the $55 Suite Team plan. Intercom Essential already includes the shared inbox, Messenger and help center.

At scale, the AI meter dominates. Using the published rates, 2,000 Fin resolutions in a month cost about $1,980 on Intercom ($0.99 x 2,000). On Zendesk, if none of those resolutions fell inside the included allowance (the allowance size was not documented in the sources reviewed), the reported $1.50 to $2.00 range would imply about $3,000 to $4,000. In practice the allowance lowers this, so treat it as a ceiling and get the actual per-resolution rate in writing.

Both approaches mean your bill rises as your AI performs better. Intercom's $0.99 is transparent but sits on top of higher seat prices; Zendesk's seat prices are lower but the AI rate is not published. Model your expected volume and resolution rate before comparing totals, and confirm current terms with both vendors.

Considerations

Intercom vs Zendesk: implementation, ecosystem, AI and security.

Ease of implementation and usability

We did not test setup or migration for either product. One independent test with a 15-agent team described Intercom as quicker for messaging-first SaaS support and Zendesk as a better fit where phone and ticket workflows are central. Both vendors sell per seat or agent with annual billing by default, so pilot one queue of real conversations in each.

Integrations and ecosystem

Intercom lists a Salesforce integration on Advanced and one guide reports 350+ integrations, with Fin able to run on other help desks. Zendesk offers email, chat, social and telephony channels, an Amazon Connect-based contact center add-on and, per one guide, a larger marketplace. Third-party integration coverage was not assessed for either product, so check each marketplace for your CRM and order systems.

AI capabilities

Both vendors sell an AI agent that resolves customer requests and an assistant for human agents. Fin is billed at $0.99 per resolution with Copilot at about $29 per seat. Zendesk bills AI agents per automated resolution beyond an allowance and sells Copilot or Advanced AI at about $50 per agent. Vendor-sponsored comparisons claim different resolution rates, but the sources reviewed do not support a neutral quality ranking, so test both on your own tickets.

Security, deployment and administration

Intercom lists SSO or SAML, custom roles and HIPAA eligibility on Expert. Zendesk lists sandboxes, audit logs and custom agent roles on Suite Enterprise. We did not review certifications, data residency or the status of any acquisition for either vendor, so request current security and contract documentation before signing.

Strengths and limitations

Intercom and Zendesk: each option, in balance.

I

Intercom

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • Fin AI Agent is available on every plan and is billed only when it resolves a conversation.
  • A chat-first suite combines inbox, Messenger and help center in one product.
  • Expert adds SSO, custom roles and HIPAA eligibility for larger or regulated teams.
Limitations
  • Per-seat prices are high compared with many help desks, and the jump from Essential to Advanced is large.
  • A second meter for Fin resolutions means the AI bill scales with support volume.
  • Sources report that a resolution can be counted after 24 hours without a customer reply, so billable outcomes may exceed explicit confirmations.
  • Reports of a rebrand and a pending Salesforce acquisition mean roadmap and pricing may change.
Z

Zendesk

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • A clear plan ladder from ticketing-only to a full omnichannel Suite.
  • AI agents are included in plans, with an allowance of automated resolutions per agent.
  • Higher tiers add governance features such as sandboxes, audit logs and custom roles.
Limitations
  • Per-agent pricing means costs rise with headcount, and monthly billing is reported about 30 percent higher.
  • AI resolutions beyond the allowance are billed per resolution, so costs vary with volume.
  • Copilot, voice, quality assurance and workforce management are priced as add-ons.
  • Enterprise pricing is quote-only, and older guides mention a Suite Growth plan that no longer appears on the main page.

Switching

Switching between Intercom and Zendesk.

Moving between Intercom and Zendesk means exporting conversations or tickets, macros and help center articles, rebuilding workflows and routing, and reconnecting channels. Because Fin can run on other help desks, some teams trial the AI layer first without replacing the help desk. Plan a parallel run on one queue and confirm your target plan includes the channels you rely on.

General switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your data and test an import into the other product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  4. Check contract terms and notice periods with both vendors.

General guidance, not specific to either vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

FAQ

Intercom vs Zendesk: questions and answers

Is Intercom or Zendesk cheaper?

Zendesk's entry plan is cheaper at about $19 per agent per month, but it is ticketing only. Intercom's Essential plan is reported at $29 to $39 per seat and includes the inbox, Messenger and help center. Total cost depends heavily on AI volume.

How does Fin pricing compare with Zendesk AI?

Fin is published at $0.99 per resolution. Zendesk includes an allowance of automated resolutions and then bills per resolution, reported at about $1.50 to $2.00, but does not display the rate on its pricing page.

Which is better for AI customer support?

The sources reviewed do not support a neutral ranking. Intercom is described as having a more polished AI agent and Zendesk as stronger on omnichannel and enterprise controls. Test both on your own conversations.

Can Intercom Fin work with Zendesk?

Yes. Fin is reported to run standalone or on other help desks, including Zendesk, so some teams add Fin without replacing their help desk.

Which is better for phone support?

Zendesk is described as stronger on telephony through Zendesk Talk, billed by usage. One guide reports voice bundled into Intercom's Advanced and Expert plans. Compare voice costs for your call volume.

Does either have a free plan?

No permanent free plan was identified for either product in the sources reviewed. Trials are available.

Which suits a small team better?

A small team that only needs a simple shared inbox may find both heavy. For ticketing at a low price Zendesk Support Team is an entry point, while Intercom suits small teams that want chat-first support and an AI agent.
