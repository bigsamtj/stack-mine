Quick answer

Notion vs Coda (Superhuman Docs): the short answer

Notion and Coda are both all-in-one workspaces that mix documents, tables and automation, but they bill differently. Notion is a docs-first workspace sold per member in Free, Plus, Business and Enterprise plans. Coda, now part of Superhuman (formerly Grammarly), is a doc-and-database hybrid sold per Doc Maker in Free, Pro, Business and Enterprise plans, with editors and viewers free.

The sources reviewed describe the products as close but different in centre of gravity. Notion is described as easier to learn and stronger for writing, wikis and community templates. Coda is described as stronger for formulas, automation buttons and interactive docs, and cheaper when many people read and few build. Notion launched Custom Agents in February 2026, and Coda's Team plan was renamed Business after Coda moved into Superhuman.

We compare what independent 2026 sources and the vendors' own pages support and mark the rest Unknown. Two sources sell competing tools. Notion's Business price appears as $15, $18 and $20 and Coda's as $30 and $33. No winner is declared.

All Coda (Superhuman Docs) plans and sources

The billing model matters more than the list price. Our arithmetic on reported annual prices for 25 members gives about $3,000 a year on Notion Plus ($10 x 25 x 12) and $5,400 on Business at $18 ($18 x 25 x 12). On Coda, if 5 of those 25 people create docs, Pro costs about $600 a year ($10 x 5 x 12) and Business about $1,800 to $1,980 ($30 to $33 x 5 x 12), because editors and viewers are free.

The gap widens with read-heavy teams. For a 100-person organisation with 20 Doc Makers, our arithmetic gives about $21,600 a year on Notion Business at $18 ($18 x 100 x 12) against about $7,920 on Coda Business at $33 ($33 x 20 x 12). If everyone builds, the two cost about the same per head.

AI placement affects totals. Two guides say full Notion AI is on Business only, while Coda includes AI credits from Pro. Prices changed on both sides and Coda was renamed after the Superhuman move, so confirm current terms with each vendor.

Considerations

Notion vs Coda (Superhuman Docs): implementation, ecosystem, AI and security.

Ease of implementation and usability

We did not test setup or migration for either product. Guides describe Notion as quicker to adopt for writing and wikis, and Coda as rewarding teams that build formula-driven docs. Rebuild one real workflow in each before choosing.

Integrations and ecosystem

Coda's Packs connect docs to external services, with one guide reporting 600+ integrations. Notion connects through its API and common business tools, with a larger template and community ecosystem. Counts were not independently verified, so check the specific tools you use.

AI capabilities

Notion bundles full AI into Business and has Custom Agents launched in February 2026, with multi-model choice. Coda includes AI credits from Pro and AI inside the doc, with enterprise search now routed through Superhuman Go. Guides lean towards Notion for AI momentum and Coda for in-doc AI. We did not test output quality.

Security, deployment and administration

Coda lists HIPAA compliance and dedicated support on Enterprise. Notion's Business and Enterprise plans add administrative controls per one guide. We did not review certifications or data residency, so request current security documentation.

Strengths and limitations

Notion and Coda (Superhuman Docs): each option, in balance.

N

Notion

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • A free plan and free guests make it easy for individuals and small teams to start.
  • Full Notion AI is reported as bundled into Business, rather than a separate add-on.
  • Flexible docs, wikis and databases suit teams that want a wiki with a light project layer.
Limitations
  • Every editor is a billed member, so seat counts can grow quickly.
  • Free and Plus include only a limited AI trial, so meaningful AI use needs Business or above.
  • Guides disagree on prices and on how AI is bundled, so budgeting needs a check of the current page.
  • Some guides note that flexible databases are lighter than purpose-built project tools for heavy operations.
CS

Coda (Superhuman Docs)

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • Only Doc Makers are billed. Editors and viewers are free on every plan, which suits organisations with many readers and few builders.
  • A doc can combine prose, tables, formulas and automations, so one tool can cover a wiki and a lightweight tracker.
  • Existing documents carry over after the rename and workspaces keep working.
  • Paid plans bundle Grammarly and Superhuman Go, adding writing and AI assistance without a separate purchase.
  • A free plan lets individuals and small groups try the product without a deadline.
Limitations
  • List prices rose with the rename: Pro moved from about $10 to $12 annually and the equivalent of Team from about $30 to $33.
  • The Doc Maker versus editor distinction adds admin overhead, and workspace admins count as paid Doc Makers.
  • The rename and move into the Superhuman suite means pricing pages, links and plan names have changed, and older articles quote outdated numbers.
  • Moving docs from an original Coda workspace into a Superhuman workspace is a manual step buyers must choose to take.
  • Per-Doc-Maker cost can exceed per-member tools such as Notion when most of the team builds docs.

Switching

Switching between Notion and Coda (Superhuman Docs).

Moving between Notion and Coda means exporting pages and tables, rebuilding databases and formulas, and recreating automations; Coda formulas and Packs have no direct Notion equivalent. Documents move more easily than structured data. Pilot one team's docs in each.

General switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your data and test an import into the other product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  4. Check contract terms and notice periods with both vendors.

General guidance, not specific to either vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

FAQ

Notion vs Coda (Superhuman Docs): questions and answers

Is Notion or Coda cheaper?

It depends on who builds. Coda charges only Doc Makers ($10 on Pro, annual), and editors and viewers are free, while Notion charges every member ($10 on Plus). For read-heavy teams Coda is cheaper; if everyone builds, they are similar.

Who owns Coda?

Coda is part of Superhuman, formerly Grammarly, and its Team plan was renamed Business in July 2026.

Which has better databases?

Guides favour Coda for formulas, automation buttons and interactive docs, and Notion for docs and wikis.

Which has better AI?

Guides lean towards Notion for AI momentum, including Custom Agents launched in February 2026, and Coda for AI inside the doc. Quality was not tested here.

Do both have free plans?

Yes. Notion's free plan has unlimited pages and free guests, and Coda's includes one Doc Maker.

Which is easier to learn?

Guides describe Notion as easier and Coda as deeper with a steeper curve.

What does Notion Business cost?

Sources report $15, $18 and $20 per member per month; $18 is commonly cited on annual billing. Confirm current terms.
