Quick answer

Linear vs Jira: the short answer

Linear and Jira are two of the most compared issue trackers for software teams. Linear is a keyboard-driven, opinionated tracker sold per user in Free, Basic, Business and Enterprise plans. Jira, from Atlassian, is a configurable work tracking product sold per user in Free, Standard, Premium and Enterprise plans, with deep links to the rest of the Atlassian suite.

The sources reviewed describe opposite philosophies. Linear is praised for speed and focus and described as the default choice for product teams without an enterprise constraint. Jira is described as the configurable enterprise standard, preferred where custom workflows, audit trails and IT service management matter, and where teams already use Confluence and other Atlassian tools.

We compare what independent 2026 sources support and mark the rest Unknown. Linear raised its prices in 2026, and Jira's rates are sliding and differ slightly by source, so we show the figures as reported. No winner is declared: the better fit depends on how much configuration you need.

Jira is slightly cheaper per user at the entry tier and the two are close at the upper tier. Our arithmetic on reported annual prices for 25 users gives about $2,373 a year on Jira Standard ($7.91 x 25 x 12) against $3,000 on Linear Basic ($10 x 25 x 12), and about $4,362 on Jira Premium ($14.54 x 25 x 12) against $4,800 on Linear Business ($16 x 25 x 12). Guides that quote Linear's earlier $8 and $14 prices show Linear as cheaper, so check which price list they used.

Jira's per-user rate slides with team size, so its real total can differ from this multiplication. Jira's cost can also rise with Marketplace apps, Atlassian Guard and companion products such as Jira Service Management, while Linear's published plans bundle more in the base plan. Linear also added metered AI credits for some features.

AI placement is contested. One guide says Linear's AI features start on Business and Jira's on Premium, while Atlassian's pricing page, as summarised in our profile, lists Rovo on paid plans. Prices and plan limits change often, so confirm current terms.

Considerations

Linear vs Jira: implementation, ecosystem, AI and security.

Ease of implementation and usability

We did not test setup or migration for either product. Guides describe Linear as quick to adopt for software teams and Jira as needing configuration for workflows, permissions and boards. One guide recommends Linear for any team without an enterprise requirement locking it to Jira, which is an opinion and not a finding.

Integrations and ecosystem

Linear connects to GitHub, Figma and Slack and supports MCP for AI coding agents. Jira connects to the Atlassian suite, including Confluence and Bitbucket, and to the Marketplace. One guide argues the Atlassian integration depth is hard to match for teams already invested. Check the specific tools you use.

AI capabilities

Linear lists AI triage, summarisation and an agent, with MCP support for codebase-aware triage per one guide. Jira lists Rovo and Atlassian Intelligence for summaries, writing help and agents. Guides lean towards Linear for AI maturity inside the issue tracker, and towards Jira for enterprise teams on Atlassian. We did not test output quality.

Security, deployment and administration

Linear lists SAML, SCIM and audit controls on Enterprise. Jira offers permissions on Standard, Atlassian Guard for SSO and SCIM, and audit features on higher tiers, with a self-managed Data Center option for organisations that need it. We did not review certifications or data residency, so request current security documentation.

Strengths and limitations

Linear and Jira: each option, in balance.

L

Linear

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • Reviewers describe a fast, opinionated interface as Linear's main differentiator for engineering teams.
  • A real free plan and short, clear paid ladder make it easy to start and budget.
  • Cycles, projects, roadmaps and a triage inbox cover the core software planning workflow.
  • Triage Intelligence and Linear MCP connect issues with AI tools and coding agents.
  • Strong GitHub, GitLab and Slack integrations link issues to code and conversations.
Limitations
  • Linear is an internal engineering tool, not a customer help desk.
  • Prices rose in 2026 and AI credits for Coding Sessions and Loops add usage-based cost.
  • The Free plan is capped at 250 issues, and Enterprise features such as SAML and audit logs need a custom plan.
  • It is cloud-only, with no self-hosted option.
  • Guest access can require paid seats, and Basic and Business are annual-commitment plans at the lowest price.
J

Jira

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • Agile boards, backlogs and roadmaps are built in, and the Free plan supports up to 10 users.
  • Rovo AI search, chat and agents are included on every paid plan, with a clear credit allowance per tier.
  • A tiered plan ladder takes teams from Free to Enterprise with multiple instances, unlimited automation and analytics.
  • Tight integration with Confluence and other Atlassian products suits teams that document in Confluence.
Limitations
  • Per-user pricing scales with team size, and reviewers describe it as expensive for very small teams.
  • Enterprise pricing is quote-only and available only on annual terms.
  • Marketplace add-ons and separately priced products such as Jira Service Management can raise total cost.
  • Published per-user prices differ between sources because of sliding tiers, so budgeting needs a current quote.

Switching

Switching between Linear and Jira.

Moving from Jira to Linear means mapping issue types and workflows to Linear's more opinionated model, importing issues, and recreating automations and custom fields that Linear may not support. Moving the other way means rebuilding workflows from scratch. Import one team's backlog, run a cycle, and compare before moving everyone.

General switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your data and test an import into the other product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  4. Check contract terms and notice periods with both vendors.

General guidance, not specific to either vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

FAQ

Linear vs Jira: questions and answers

Is Linear or Jira cheaper?

Jira is slightly cheaper at the entry tier: about $7.91 per user per month against $10 for Linear Basic, billed annually. Guides quoting Linear's earlier $8 price show the reverse, so check the date.

Do Linear and Jira have free plans?

Yes. Linear's free plan is capped at 250 issues. Jira Free supports up to 10 users.

Which is faster to use?

Guides describe Linear as the faster, more focused tool, and Jira as more configurable but heavier.

Which has better AI features?

Guides lean towards Linear for AI inside the issue tracker and Jira for enterprise teams on Atlassian. Sources disagree on which Jira plans include AI, so verify.

Can I self-host Linear or Jira?

Jira offers a self-managed Data Center option. Linear is cloud only.

Which is better for enterprise?

Jira is described as the configurable enterprise standard with audit trails and ITSM integration. Linear offers SAML, SCIM and audit controls on Enterprise.

Can I migrate from Jira to Linear?

Yes, guides describe importing issues, though workflows and automations need rebuilding in Linear's more opinionated model.
