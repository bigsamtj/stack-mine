Quick answer

Airtable vs monday.com: the short answer

Airtable and monday.com are both visual tools that non-developers use to run projects and processes, but they differ in design. Airtable is a relational database with spreadsheet-like views, automation and interfaces, sold per editor in Free, Team, Business and Enterprise Scale plans. monday.com is a board-based work management platform sold per seat in a free plan and four paid plans, with a three-seat minimum on paid plans.

The sources reviewed describe the choice as data against work. Airtable is described as stronger for structured, relational data and for high automation volumes per dollar, with no seat minimum. monday.com is described as better for project management, with visual boards, dashboards, native integrations and an easier learning curve.

We compare what independent 2026 sources support and mark the rest Unknown. Two sources are written by vendors of competing tools, and one is monday.com's own blog. Sources disagree on the date monday.com's AI credits became mandatory. No winner is declared: the better fit depends on whether your work is a database or a project.

All monday.com plans and sources

monday.com is cheaper per seat at the entry and mid tiers. Our arithmetic on reported annual prices for 25 users gives about $3,600 a year on monday.com Standard ($12 x 25 x 12) and $5,700 on Pro ($19 x 25 x 12), against $6,000 on Airtable Team ($20 x 25 x 12) and $13,500 on Business ($45 x 25 x 12). At Pro's $20 figure the total would be about $6,000.

Seat counting changes the picture. Airtable bills only editors and has no seat minimum, so an organisation with 8 editors and many readers pays about $1,920 a year on Team ($20 x 8 x 12), while monday.com bills every seat. Automation volume also favours Airtable on a per-dollar basis, with 25,000 runs on Team against 250 on monday.com Standard.

AI changes the totals. One guide reports monday.com AI credits became mandatory, with a reported 3,000-credit monthly minimum on Pro, while Airtable's Omni AI is reported free. The date differs by source, so confirm current terms with both vendors.

Considerations

Airtable vs monday.com: implementation, ecosystem, AI and security.

Ease of implementation and usability

We did not test setup or migration for either product. Guides describe monday.com as easier to roll out for non-technical teams and Airtable as needing database thinking for linked records. Pilot one real workflow in each.

Integrations and ecosystem

One guide describes monday.com as having 200+ native integrations out of the box, including Microsoft Teams, while Airtable connects through its API, synced databases and Zapier, with some syncs gated to Business and above. Counts were not independently verified, so check the specific tools you use.

AI capabilities

Airtable describes itself as AI-native, with Omni AI for building apps, Field Agents and a Hyperagent launched in April 2026. monday.com has AI agents and an assistant with mandatory credits. We did not test output quality for either product.

Security, deployment and administration

Airtable lists an admin panel and SAML SSO on Business and sales-led Enterprise Scale. monday.com positions Enterprise for governance, security and scale on a quote basis. We did not review certifications or data residency, so request current security documentation.

Strengths and limitations

Airtable and monday.com: each option, in balance.

A

Airtable

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • Billing is per editor, and Airtable states that read-only collaborators, form submissions and share links are not charged on Team or Business.
  • A Free plan and a $20 Team plan let small teams start without a sales conversation, and Business can be bought online.
  • The relational model with several views over the same data is flexible enough to replace a spreadsheet, a tracker and a simple CRM.
  • Limits are enforced gently: Airtable says it will never remove data when you reach record or attachment limits.
  • AI credits and automations are included in the plan structure rather than sold only as separate products.
Limitations
  • Records per base are the most common upgrade trigger: guides report a 50,000 record ceiling on Team, which a busy CRM-style base can reach well before the team grows.
  • Per-editor pricing can become expensive when many people need to edit, and Business costs more than double Team per seat.
  • Hitting record or attachment limits blocks new records until you upgrade, and automation and API limits cap activity at plan level.
  • It is a build-your-own platform, so it needs setup and design time that a purpose-built project tool does not.
  • Enterprise Scale pricing is not published and third-party estimates differ.
MC

monday.com

Strengths and limitations from the profile

Strengths
  • A free plan and clear Basic, Standard and Pro steps make it easy to start small.
  • Timeline, calendar and guest access arrive at the mid-priced Standard plan.
  • Automation allowances scale from 250 to 25,000 actions a month by plan.
Limitations
  • Paid plans require a minimum of three seats, which penalises very small teams.
  • Enterprise pricing is quote-based and reported to carry a minimum 12-month term.
  • monday CRM, dev and service are priced separately, so multi-product use costs more.
  • The free plan is limited to two seats and three boards in at least one source.

Switching

Switching between Airtable and monday.com.

Moving between Airtable and monday.com means exporting bases to CSV or boards to Excel, rebuilding relations, formulas and automations, and recreating views. Linked-record structure rarely survives an export. Rebuild one workflow in the other tool before migrating the rest.

General switching checklist
  1. Export a sample of your data and test an import into the other product.
  2. List the automations, integrations and permissions you would need to rebuild.
  3. Pilot with one team before a full cut-over.
  4. Check contract terms and notice periods with both vendors.

General guidance, not specific to either vendor. Confirm migration options with the vendors.

FAQ

Airtable vs monday.com: questions and answers

Is Airtable or monday.com cheaper?

monday.com is cheaper per seat: Standard is $12 against $20 for Airtable Team, billed annually. Airtable bills only editors, which can narrow the gap for read-heavy teams.

Does monday.com have a seat minimum?

Yes, three seats on paid plans. Airtable has no seat minimum.

Which has the better free plan?

Airtable's free plan is reported at up to 5 editors and about 1,000 records per base. monday.com's covers two seats.

Which has better automation?

Guides report Airtable offers far more automation per dollar, with 25,000 runs on Team against 250 on monday.com Standard.

Which is better for project management?

Guides describe monday.com as the better fit for project management and Airtable for relational data and custom apps.

Do both have AI?

Yes. Airtable lists AI on paid plans with Omni AI reported free, and monday.com has AI agents with mandatory credits. Quality was not tested here.

Which is easier to learn?

Guides describe monday.com as more intuitive and Airtable as demanding database thinking.
