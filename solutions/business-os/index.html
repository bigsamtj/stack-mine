Built for Indian regulations

Compliance built into Business OS.

Business OS is configured for the rules Indian SMEs run under in 2026, and reviewed whenever they change.

GST e-invoicing and the 30-day rule

E-invoicing is mandatory once aggregate turnover crosses ₹5 crore in any year since 2017-18. Businesses at ₹10 crore and above must report invoices to the IRP within 30 days of the invoice date (since 1 April 2025).

How Business OS handles itIRN and QR are generated from the invoice screen, and invoices nearing the 30-day limit are flagged before the portal would reject them.

E-way bills

Goods movement above the threshold needs an e-way bill, and Part A can be filled from e-invoice data.

How Business OS handles itE-way bills are created from the same dispatch, with vehicle updates and validity alerts.

Companies Act audit trail

Companies must use accounting software that records an edit log of every change, which cannot be disabled (since 1 April 2023).

How Business OS handles itEvery create, edit and delete is logged with user, time and the before-and-after value.

New labour codes

The four labour codes took effect on 21 November 2025. The uniform wage definition means basic pay must generally be at least 50% of total pay, which changes PF, gratuity and bonus bases. State rules are still being finalised.

How Business OS handles itSalary structures, wage-base checks and PF, ESI and PT rules are configurable per state, so payroll can follow each state's notification.

Income-tax Act, 2025

From 1 April 2026, TDS on salary sits under section 392 and other TDS under section 393, with new return and certificate forms (for example Form 138 replacing Form 24Q and Form 130 replacing Form 16).

How Business OS handles itTDS working, challans and returns follow the new section and form references.

MSME 45-day payments

Payments to registered micro and small suppliers are due within 45 days under the MSMED Act, and late dues can lose their income-tax deduction.

How Business OS handles itVendors carry an MSME flag, and payables age against the 45-day limit with alerts before it is crossed.

This summary is for planning and is not legal or tax advice. Thresholds and dates change, so confirm current requirements with your CA or legal adviser. We update configurations when rules change under the Stack18 Care plan.

FAQ

Business OS questions, answered.

Pricing, compliance, data and timelines. If your question is not here, a senior specialist will answer it on a free 30-minute call.

Talk to Stack18
We use Tally. Do we have to stop?

No. We migrate your masters and history into Business OS, and if your CA prefers Tally for filing, the Tally sync keeps both in step.

Is there a per-user fee?

No. You pay a one-time build and setup fee per module. Add as many users as you need.

Does payroll handle Indian statutory deductions?

Yes. PF, ESI, professional tax and TDS are computed from attendance and salary structures, with challan-ready reports.

Can it run on mobile?

Yes. The owner dashboard, approvals, attendance, field sales and employee self-service are designed for phones first.

How long does it take to go live?

The Launch edition typically goes live in 3–4 weeks. Larger editions run in phases so the business keeps running while each part comes online.

Where is our data stored?

On your own cloud account in India, or on your servers.

How much does ERP software cost for an Indian SME?

Business OS editions cost ₹2.8L (Launch), ₹5.7L (Grow) and ₹9.95L (Scale) as a one-time build and setup, excluding GST, with no per-user fees. Hosting is paid at cost on your own cloud, and an optional Care plan starts at 15% of build value a year.

Is Business OS ready for the new labour codes?

Yes. Salary structures, the wage definition used for PF, gratuity and bonus, and state-wise PT and labour welfare rules are configurable, so payroll can be aligned as each state notifies its rules.

Does it handle e-invoicing and the 30-day reporting limit?

Yes. IRN and QR codes are generated from the invoice, e-way bills from the dispatch, and invoices close to the 30-day IRP reporting window are flagged for businesses with turnover of ₹10 crore or more.

Does Business OS keep a Companies Act audit trail?

Yes. Every change to a transaction or master is logged with the user, time and old and new values, and the log cannot be switched off.

Can we start small and add modules later?

Yes. Most businesses start with Launch and add CRM, payroll or manufacturing later. Modules you already own are never charged again.

Who is Business OS not right for?

Very small businesses that only need billing are usually better served by an off-the-shelf accounting tool. Large groups with complex consolidation may need an enterprise ERP or a custom build.
