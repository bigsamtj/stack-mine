Overview

What mobile forensics covers.

Extract meaning from mobile evidence, lawfully.

Mobile devices contain communications, location, application and account evidence that may exist nowhere else. They also present encryption, access controls and platform-specific artefact structures that require specialist handling.

Examinations are conducted within lawful authority, with documented methods and reporting that separates what the evidence shows from what is inferred.

Approach

A disciplined engagement sequence.

The sequence below is adapted to the mandate. Authorisation and safety controls are never optional.

  1. Instruction and authority

    Establish the legal or organisational authority, questions to answer, constraints and reporting requirements.

  2. Acquisition and preservation

    Collect data using documented, forensically sound methods, with integrity verification and chain-of-custody records.

  3. Analysis and correlation

    Examine artefacts, build timelines, correlate sources and test competing explanations.

  4. Reporting and support

    Produce a clear, defensible report. Provide testimony or further analysis only where agreed and lawful.

Engagement boundary

HELIOS SYSTEMS does not claim certifications, audit attestations or legal outcomes that have not been agreed. Findings and recommendations are provided within the scope and limitations documented in the engagement.

Questions

Mobile Forensics: common questions.

Can you recover deleted data?

Recovery depends on the device, file system, use since deletion and preservation status. Acquisition first prevents further loss; the realistic recovery scope is stated after examination.

Will the evidence be admissible in court?

Admissibility depends on jurisdiction and procedure. HELIOS SYSTEMS follows documented acquisition and preservation practices and provides expert reporting; it does not guarantee a court outcome.
